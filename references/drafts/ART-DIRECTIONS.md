# The Carcass, round 3, step 1: three art directions

Test place: **The Breath**. Format assumed: 16:9.

Why The Breath is the hardest place: the other five body parts are solid things with an obvious landform (ribs are arches, the spine is a ridge, the heart is a chamber, the marsh is a marsh, the eye is a lake). A lung is air. The Breath has to show air as a place you can travel through and as the thing that kills you, which invites exactly the flat fog painting that failed in rounds 1 and 2. If a direction can make The Breath read as a real location, it can carry the other five.

Each direction below is a rule set for the whole film, shown as one establishing view and two detail views of the same location. Nothing from rounds 1 or 2 was reused. The nine kept images are in `step-1-kept/`; model, prompt and references for each are in `NOTES.md`.

## A. Overgrowth

The rule: the body is buried. The world that grew over the titan has won, and the anatomy survives only as architecture inside warm, wet forest. Daylight filtered through cloud, canopy and cave openings. Deep green, wet black rock, pale bone, a little dark red. Life is abundant and looks gentle. The killer is always small and quiet: spores, sap, roots, parasites. The narrator's register: it looks like shelter.

The Breath in this direction, the wind cave: the titan's windpipe, now a cave passage about two hundred metres high. Part of the roof fell in long ago; daylight comes down the hole, a waterfall with it, and rainforest has grown on the rubble. A river runs the length of the passage and is the camera's route. The cartilage rings survive as pale bands in the rock: one across the roof, one fallen across the floor. A herd of eyeless long-necked grazers drinks in the shallows.

How it kills you: pale bladders grow from the bones of the last herd and vent a gold dust. It roots in whatever breathes it. The herd is drinking beside its own dead, and the bladders are already sprouting on one animal's back.

Camera route: in along the river from the dark passage; past the bones on the near bank (detail 1); across the shallows to the herd (detail 2); on under the fallen ring toward the second skylight.

The other five places under this rule:
- The Ribs: a jungle gorge bridged by rib arches hung with vines and aerial roots. The vines are not vines.
- The Heart: a sunken basin of hot red pools and steam in the forest, ringed by flowers that feed on the heat. The ground under the leaf litter is scalding.
- The Blood Marsh: a black-water swamp forest stained red, buttress roots and floating flowers. What drinks here drinks you.
- The Spine: a cloud-forest ridge of vertebra peaks with waterfalls. The moss lets go.
- The Eye: a single round lake under a ring of forest, dark in the middle. Something in it watches.
- The reveal: a green continent. The body has to be read from ridges and river systems under forest and cloud, which is the hardest of the three reveals to make read.

Risks: it is the most Earth-like of the three (a rainforest cave with a herd); the alien has to be carried by the animals and the scale. Six green places can blur together. It is the heaviest to build: dense overlapping foliage and close animal skin.

## B. Titan-fall

The rule: the body is the land. The anatomy lies bare as geology at continental scale, worn by sea and weather. Life is sparse and specialised and gathers only where the body still leaks something: vapour, heat, blood, brine. Low sun through fog and vapour, long shadows, backlight. Ivory rock and teal water are the base, and each place gets one saturated colour of its own, carried by what lives there. The killer is the place itself, plain physics (air, heat, tide, mud, lightning, depth), and it is always visible in frame as a line you should not cross.

The Breath in this direction, the Bellows: the titan's lung, worn by the sea into a tidal shelf of pale bone-rock on a cold coast at dawn. The shelf is pierced by the airways: shafts forty metres across with ruined cartilage hoops down their walls. Cold white vapour wells out of them and, being heavy, slides away low across the rock. Wine-red gill-plumes grow only where the vapour is. Bone-white waders with a tuft of feeding tendrils instead of a head work the tide channel, which is the camera's route. Kilometres off, fog pours from a cave in the sea cliff.

How it kills you: the air the shafts breathe out is heavier than yours and has nothing in it to breathe. It pools on the shelf at dawn. Whatever wanders to the rim lies down beside the last one that did.

Camera route: low along the tide channel through the feeding waders (detail 2); bank right onto the dry rock at the rim, the carcass and the animal standing over the drop (detail 1); over the lip and down the shaft past the hoops, or away across the flats toward the cave.

The other five places under this rule, each with its own colour, weather and colony life so that the film never returns to round 1's uniform grey rock:
- The Ribs: rib arches hundreds of metres high standing in a tidal strait like sea stacks, hung with dark weed and nesting colonies. Slate-green sea, white surf, rust lichen. The tide comes through the cage as a wall.
- The Heart: the collapsed chamber is a caldera, and the body's last heat is a lake of slow lava under a black crust that things walk on. Black, ember orange, sulphur yellow. The crust looks like ground.
- The Blood Marsh: brine flats where the old blood still seeps up and the pools bubble. Oxblood water, white salt crust, crimson mats. The mud holds what steps on it.
- The Spine: a knife ridge of vertebrae above the cloud in a standing storm. Blue-grey, snow, violet light. The lightning walks the ridge from spire to spire.
- The Eye: a perfectly round lake in a socket of cliffs: a ring of pale shallows and a black centre that goes all the way down. Black-blue and amber. What rises from the centre.
- The reveal: a pale skeleton of a continent in a dark sea, the ribs a row of capes, under real cloud bands. The body shape reads at once because the bone is bare.

Risks: it is the closest of the three to the fault of round 1. Pale rock fills most of this frame. The guard is the rule above: one saturated colour and one colony of life per place, and no two places share a palette or a weather.

## C. Corpse-light

The rule: the body still glows. It is always dusk. The land is dark, and apart from the last band of sky the only light is made by what lives on the body. Every light is bait or a warning. Blue-black land, cyan-green living light, amber, a dull orange band at the horizon. The killer is the lure: you die walking toward the light.

The Breath in this direction, the lantern field: the lung has weathered open into a long valley of dark ash. Its air sacs are still alive and grow in colonies along one stream, from the size of a barrel to the size of a house, lit dimly from inside. More of them glow in a cave in the cliff. Heavy banded eyeless animals come down the valley to them. The stream is the camera's route.

How it kills you: the sacs glow to bring animals in. The threads around the base take the legs first. What the light is for is inside the largest sac.

Camera route: up the stream to the near colony and the thing inside the veined sac (detail 1); on to the middle colony and the animal already caught by the legs (detail 2); then up the scree to the lit cave.

The other five places under this rule:
- The Ribs: rib arches against the last light, hung with curtains of glowing threads like fishing lines.
- The Heart: the one warm light in the film, a lake of lava glow in a black basin with swarms circling it.
- The Blood Marsh: black water with drifting red and blue lights that lead across ground that does not hold.
- The Spine: a storm ridge lit by lightning and by cold fire on the peaks.
- The Eye: a round lake lit from beneath. The light moves to follow you.
- The reveal: the night side from orbit, the body drawn in living light with the day line crossing it. It reads at real scale the way city lights do from orbit.

Risks: six dark places can look alike. Dark frames with fine glows are the kind of picture that video compression treats worst (stated from general knowledge of how compression behaves; not tested on X in this run). Every glow has to light its surroundings for real or it reads as a painting.

## Which I would choose

**B, Titan-fall.**

- It is the one where the place reads as three-dimensional from the landform alone. The channel, the shaft, the flats and the cliff cave sit at four distances, and each is somewhere a camera can go.
- It is the most alien of the three and the hardest to mistake for Earth, and the death is in the frame: you can see the line the waders should not cross.
- It gives the strongest reveal. Bare bone in a dark sea reads as a body from orbit without help.
- It is the one I am most confident I can match in three.js (stated, not measured; see below): terrain and hero rock forms, moving vapour, wet rock and water, plumes in wind, and flocking waders with simple bodies, rather than dense forest or a film lit only by glows.

What it costs: B has the least life per frame and the most bare rock. If the film should feel lush first and deadly second, A is the better pick; if it should feel like one long night, C is. C is my second choice.

## Build approach

Nothing was built in this round, as instructed. Labels: MEASURED means tested; STATED means an estimate or a claim; UNKNOWN means not known.

- MEASURED (your rendering capability study of 19 November 2026, not re-measured in this run): the sandbox shows no GPU, and a shadowed scene of 200,000 triangles at 1080p rendered in software at about 1.3 seconds per frame.
- STATED: two critics were shown the nine pictures as engineers and asked how closely each could be matched in three.js rendered offline (MATCH, 0 to 10). GPT-6.2 Sol: A 6, B 8, C 7, would build B. Claude Opus 5.5: A 3, B 5, C 5, would build C then B. Both put A last. These are opinions from stills, not tests.
- STATED, how I would build each. B: sculpted height-field terrain plus hero meshes for the shafts and hoops, a flowing water surface, ray-marched vapour, instanced plumes moved by a wind shader, and procedurally animated waders in flocks. A: a cave shell, very heavy instanced vegetation, volumetric daylight, particle waterfall and spray, and skinned animals with wet skin. C: dark terrain, translucent emissive sacs with real light transport onto the ground and mist, fog volumes, and insect particles.
- UNKNOWN: whether any of the three can be matched to this bar at 4K in three.js; the frame time for volumetric vapour, dense foliage or many small lights in software; how convincing procedurally animated animals will be in close-up. None of this can be measured until building is allowed. Round 3 builds nothing, so these stay unknown through step 2, and the first thing the build should do is test each one small.

## How step 2 would run

For each of the six places: one establishing view, three or four detail views, and one top-down layout sketch with the detail positions and the camera route; then the reveal. The method that worked here carries over: every picture is a fresh generation, a real photograph is supplied only as the standard for how a camera records light, and each detail is generated from its establishing view as the same place at the same moment. The change for step 2 is to lock each animal's design, each carcass position and each prop layout in the establishing view first, so that the details cannot drift.
