THE CHANGE. The inside wall of the shaft shows three cartilage hoops one below another, like the rings inside a windpipe: each is a thick, smooth, rounded band of ivory-white cartilage, as tall as a house, that bulges from the rock wall and follows it all the way round the shaft, seen through thin vapour. They are wet, slightly translucent at their edges like horn, uneven in thickness and sagging, with tea-coloured drip stains. The top one is broken through in one place and its fallen pieces lie on the one below. Below the third hoop the shaft is dark. They are grown, not built: part of the wall, never free-standing, nothing like pipes, beams or masonry. The crimson growths at the lip become soft wet feathery gill-plumes that hang inward over the drop and lift in the rising vapour.
