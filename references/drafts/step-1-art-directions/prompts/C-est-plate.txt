Two reference images are supplied, with different jobs.
Image 1 is a real photograph. Use it only as the standard for how the new picture is rendered: how a real camera records light, haze, exposure and colour; soft highlights; detail that softens with distance; large plain, unremarkable surfaces. Do not copy its subject, its layout or anything in it.
Image 2 is a rough, blurred layout sketch of the place to photograph. Use it only for where things are: the viewpoint, the horizon, the route, the position and size of each landmark. Do not copy its surface texture, its sharpness or its rendering. Invent every surface fresh, as a camera would record real materials.

Make a new 16:9 photograph: one frame from a big-budget natural-history film about an alien world, shot on location with a large-format cinema camera and a fast 24 mm lens. It must be indistinguishable from real location photography.

THE PLACE. Late dusk in a long shallow valley on the body of a creature the size of a mountain range that died ages ago. Its lung has weathered open here. The ground is matte dark volcanic ash and scree with patches of grey lichen, dry except along one narrow stream that runs from the bottom of the frame away down the valley: the route. Along the stream and its seeps grow the creature's air sacs, still alive: soft membrane bladders from the size of a barrel to the size of a house, alone or in bunches of two to five, each a different kind of skin: one milky and smooth, one ribbed like a gourd, one dark and leathery with a single glowing split, one thin and clear with veins, several dead, collapsed and unlit. Luminous organisms live in the living ones, so they glow from within, cold blue-green, dimly and unevenly, and each lit sac throws a soft pool of light onto the ground, the stream and the mist around it, fading quickly with distance. There are few of them and they are far apart, with wide stretches of dark ground between. On the right the valley side is a plain weathered cliff with scree at its foot and one low cave from which a faint glow and a slow tongue of mist come out along the ground. Far down the valley, where it bends, a wider, dimmer glow on low mist shows that the field goes on out of sight.

LIFE. One heavy eyeless animal with dark and pale bands, in the middle distance, seen from three-quarters behind as it walks toward a lit sac, no taller than a twenty-fifth of the frame height; two more of its kind far down the valley, almost lost in the dusk. A loose swarm of tiny pale flyers around the nearest light only.

LIGHT. Exposed like Image 1: real dusk. The sky holds the last light, deep blue-grey under thin cloud with a narrow band of dull orange afterglow at the horizon, no stars. The land is dark but its forms are readable in the cold skylight. The glows are small, soft and not overexposed. Colours: blue-black land, cyan-green living light, a little amber, the dull orange band in the sky.

WHAT MAKES IT REAL. One light source and one weather, consistent everywhere. Most of the frame is plain: dark matte ground, mist and sky. Surfaces have no repeating pattern, no scales, no crackle and no carved detail. Nothing is evenly spaced and nothing is repeated; every object differs in size, shape and state, and some are broken, half hidden or cut off by the edge of the frame. Animals are small in the frame, in different poses and facing different ways, some partly hidden; none is posed side-on for the camera. Far things are paler, softer and lower in contrast than near things. Natural exposure with gentle highlights and open shadows, no added sharpening, no glow added in post, restrained colour, fine film grain.

The land plainly continues beyond every edge of the frame. No people, no buildings, paths or railings, no equipment, no text, no watermark, no border.
