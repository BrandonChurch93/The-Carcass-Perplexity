A wide 16:9 landscape frame from a big-budget natural-history film about an alien world. It must read as real night-time location photography, not artwork: fast 24 mm lens at high sensitivity, available light only, true lens perspective, fine physical surface detail where light reaches it, mist thickening with distance, visible sensor noise in the shadows.

THE PLACE. Deep dusk in a shallow valley on the body of a creature the size of a mountain range that died ages ago. Its lung has weathered open here, and the valley floor is crowded with its air sacs, still alive: thousands of soft membrane bladders pressed together in heaps like frogspawn or clustered puffballs, joined by ropes of wet tissue, from the size of a barrel to the size of a house. None is a neat dome: they sag, wrinkle, bulge and lean on each other. Their skins are thin, leathery and wet, with a few branching veins, pale where stretched and dark where slack. They breathe: at this moment some are swollen tight and others have collapsed into folds. Colonies of luminous organisms live inside them, so each sac glows faintly from within, cold blue-green with a dim amber core, unevenly. Inside several, against the light, hang the small dark shapes of caught animals with too many thin limbs, half dissolved. The sacs exhale a low mist through puckered pores; it lies knee-deep over the ground, catches their light and drains slowly downhill.

VIEWPOINT AND LAYOUT, asymmetric, nothing centred. Seen from standing height on a wet black rock path. The path runs from the bottom right away down the valley between the heaps of sacs, beside a narrow black stream that mirrors the glow: the obvious route into the picture. No single sac dominates: the nearest heap is on the left, four or five sacs the size of small cars within arm's reach, near enough to see their veins, the beads of moisture on them and one jet of mist leaving a pore. The heaps recede down the valley at every distance, dimmer and bluer in the mist, hundreds of soft lights, climbing the far slope. Small pale moth-like animals spiral toward several of them. The valley is closed at the far end by a dark cliff pocked with ragged cave mouths that breathe out mist. The sky is the last band of deep blue and dull orange twilight low on the horizon, with the first stars above.

LIGHT AND EXPOSURE. Exposed for the living light, so most of the frame is dark: blue-black land, soft cyan-green pools of light on mist and wet rock around each heap, a few amber points. The glow is weak, falls off quickly and is never even. The mood is silent, beautiful and lethal.
