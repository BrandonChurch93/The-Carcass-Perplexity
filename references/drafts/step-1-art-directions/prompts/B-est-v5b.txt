A wide 16:9 landscape frame from a big-budget natural-history film about an alien world, shot on location. It must read as real photography of a real place, not artwork: 28 mm lens, deep focus, true lens perspective, natural light only, sea fog building with distance, slight film grain, restrained natural colour.

THE PLACE. A vast tidal shelf on a cold coast at low tide, early morning, sea fog burning off. The shelf is the exposed lung of a creature the size of a mountain range that died ages ago, worn down by the sea into a wave-cut platform: pale grey-white bone-rock planed almost flat, smooth and wet, in broad low steps a hand high, finely cracked, holding thin sheets of standing water; it is porous and honeycombed only where an edge has broken. The shelf is pierced by the creature's airways: round shafts that drop straight down into the dark, flush with the ground like sinkholes, from one metre to sixty metres across, their lips broken and undercut. The inside wall of each shaft is ringed with hoops of ivory cartilage, one below another, like the inside of a hose. The ocean swell drives air through them from below and they all breathe out together: cold white vapour wells up out of every shaft and leans the same way on the wind; it is heavy, and after a short rise it sinks and slides away low across the rock. Life here is animal, not plant, and it lives only where the breath and the water are: deep crimson feathery gill-plumes in a ragged ring just inside the lip of each shaft, and in a narrow band along the two edges of the one tide channel. Everything else is bare wet rock and water.

VIEWPOINT AND LAYOUT, asymmetric. Seen from a low outcrop about twelve metres above the shelf. One shallow tide channel of pale teal water runs from the bottom centre of the frame away across the shelf in a slow S: the route. On the right, eighty metres away, the nearest large shaft, forty metres across: from here we look partly down into it and see its broken lip, the first three cartilage hoops of its far inside wall lit by the low sun, the darkness below them, crimson plumes inside the lip pressed flat by the rush of air, and its vapour pouring up and leaning away to the left, thin enough near the lip that the ringed far wall shows through it. In the lower left corner the wet edge of the outcrop, plain rock. Beyond, at very different distances and off any line, a handful of smaller shafts, each a dark hole with its own leaning column, one of them quiet with only a wisp; far out, dozens of thin columns dissolve into a bank of fog. Four hundred metres away, where the channel widens into shallows, a loose flock of several hundred small bone-white wading animals feeds, scattered unevenly in clumps and stragglers across the shallows, never in a line, tiny from here, with their reflections. They are not birds: each stands on four thin stilt legs and has no long neck. A few dozen have taken off and fly low in a ragged group. At the far end, kilometres away and half lost in fog, the coast rises into snow-streaked hills that end in a dark cliff with one enormous round cave mouth, from which a slow river of fog pours down onto the shelf.

RESTRAINT. This is a calm, wide establishing frame. The land and the weather are the subject. Big simple shapes; wide areas of plain wet grey-white rock, shallow water and fog. Life is sparse and small in the frame and sits only in the zones named above, never spread over the rock. There is one accent colour, the crimson, and it covers very little of the frame. Nothing is repeated: every shaft, vapour column and patch of growth differs from the others in size, shape, state and angle, and some are broken, some empty, some half hidden.

LIGHT AND WEATHER. Backlit. The sun is low, ahead and to the left, just outside the frame; its light comes through the fog as a broad pale-gold glow. Everything facing the viewer is in soft cool shadow with bright warm rims; the vapour columns glow where the sun passes through them and throw long soft shadows toward the viewer across the wet rock; a path of sun glitter lies on the channel and the standing water. Colours: grey-white and ivory rock, pale teal water, a little deep crimson, wet black in the shafts, pale gold light. The mood is cold, quiet and lethal.

It must feel like a place you could walk into and keep walking: firm ground at your feet, a readable route into the middle distance, far landmarks dissolving in the air, the land plainly continuing beyond every edge of the frame. Nothing posed, nothing centred, no painterly brushwork, no illustration or game-art styling. No people, no buildings or ruins, no cameras, drones or equipment, no text, no watermark, no border.
