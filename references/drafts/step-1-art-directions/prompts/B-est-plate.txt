Two reference images are supplied, with different jobs.
Image 1 is a real photograph. Use it only as the standard for how the new picture is rendered: how a real camera records light, haze, exposure and colour; soft highlights; detail that softens with distance; large plain, unremarkable surfaces. Do not copy its subject, its layout or anything in it.
Image 2 is a rough, blurred layout sketch of the place to photograph. Use it only for where things are: the viewpoint, the horizon, the route, the position and size of each landmark. Do not copy its surface texture, its sharpness or its rendering. Invent every surface fresh, as a camera would record real materials.

Make a new 16:9 photograph: one frame from a big-budget natural-history film about an alien world, shot on location with a large-format cinema camera and a 28 mm lens. It must be indistinguishable from real location photography.

THE PLACE. A vast tidal shelf on a cold coast at low tide, early morning, sea fog burning off. The shelf is the exposed lung of a creature the size of a mountain range that died ages ago, worn down by the sea into a wave-cut platform of pale grey-white bone-rock: smooth, water-worn, planed nearly flat in broad low steps, with a few long joints, shallow pools, wet patches and dry patches, and drifts of shingle in the hollows, like a real limestone shore. One shallow tide channel of pale teal water winds away from the bottom of the frame across the shelf: the route. On the right, about eighty metres away, the shelf is pierced by one of the creature's airways: a shaft forty metres across that drops straight down into the dark, like a collapsed sinkhole with an irregular, broken, partly undercut lip. Its inside wall shows three thick ivory cartilage hoops one below another, uneven in thickness, sagging, stained with rust-coloured mineral streaks, the top one broken through with rubble lying on the one below. Cold white vapour wells up out of the shaft, leans on the wind and, being heavy, sinks and slides away low across the rock. Just inside the lip, where the vapour is, grow deep crimson feathery gill-plumes in three ragged clumps of different sizes; nothing grows anywhere else on the shelf except a thin dark line of weed at the waterline of the channel. Farther off, at different distances and off any line, two smaller shafts show only as dark holes with their own thin leaning vapour, and far out one tall column of vapour stands against the fog. Kilometres away the coast rises to an ordinary weathered sea cliff with level beds, scree and snow lying in its gullies; in it is one very large ragged cave mouth, wider than it is tall, and a slow river of fog pours out of it and down onto the shelf.

LIFE. Bone-white wading animals, each on four thin stilt legs with a long neck that ends in a tuft of feeding tendrils instead of a head: one small loose group far down the channel, a few stragglers at different distances, one standing alone near the shaft, and a far flock like scattered grains of rice on the shallows, a few of them in the air. The nearest is no taller than a twenty-fifth of the frame height.

LIGHT. Backlit by a low sun that is outside the frame to the upper left; no sun disc is visible. A broad pale-gold glow comes through the fog. Everything facing the viewer is in soft cool shadow with warm rims; the vapour glows where the light passes through it; sun glitter lies on the channel and the wet rock. Colours: grey-white and ivory rock, pale teal water, a little deep crimson, pale gold light.

WHAT MAKES IT REAL. One light source and one weather, consistent everywhere. Most of the frame is plain: bare pale rock, shallow water, fog and sky. Surfaces have no repeating pattern, no scales, no crackle and no carved detail. Nothing is evenly spaced and nothing is repeated; every object differs in size, shape and state, and some are broken, half hidden or cut off by the edge of the frame. Animals are small in the frame, in different poses and facing different ways, some partly hidden; none is posed side-on for the camera. Far things are paler, softer and lower in contrast than near things. Natural exposure with gentle highlights and open shadows, no added sharpening, no glow added in post, restrained colour, fine film grain.

The land plainly continues beyond every edge of the frame. No people, no buildings, paths or railings, no equipment, no text, no watermark, no border.
