A wide 16:9 landscape frame from a big-budget natural-history film about an alien world. It must read as real location photography, not artwork: 24 mm lens, deep focus, natural light only, true lens perspective, fine physical surface detail, sea haze building with distance, slight film grain.

THE PLACE. A tidal shelf on a cold coast at low tide, soon after sunrise, sea fog burning off. The shelf is the exposed lung of a creature the size of a mountain range that died ages ago: bone-white, porous and honeycombed like weathered coral limestone, tide-stained and streaked with droppings. It is completely overgrown with animal life, not plants: wide carpets of crimson feathery gill-plumes like the crowns of giant tube worms, beds of pale barnacle-like cones, black-green ribbon weed, orange crusts. Set into the shelf are the open ends of the airways: enormous round throats twenty to sixty metres across, each a low broken collar of ivory cartilage built from stacked rings, like the cut end of a giant ribbed hose, none taller than a house, all tilted at slightly different angles. Every throat is exhaling at the same moment: a dense column of cold white vapour pours up from each and leans the same way on the wind.

VIEWPOINT AND LAYOUT, asymmetric, nothing centred. Seen from standing height at the edge of a tide channel. The channel of pale teal seawater runs from the bottom centre away across the shelf, bending between the throats toward the far distance: the obvious route into the picture. On the right, about forty metres away, the nearest throat: its wet ringed rim, the dark opening, crimson plumes crowding the rim and bending outward in the rush of air. On the left bank in the foreground the crimson plume carpet comes right up to the viewer's feet, wet and glistening. More than twenty other exhaling throats stand at every distance along the channel, shrinking and paling into the fog. In the middle distance four tall pale animals on very long thin stilt legs wade the channel in a line, small beside the throats; a flock of hundreds of small dark flyers wheels through one distant column. At the far end of the channel, huge and faint in the fog, the broken end of the main windpipe lies against a headland like a ringed tunnel mouth hundreds of metres high, breathing out the fog bank itself.

LIGHT AND WEATHER. A low sun, back left, just breaking through the fog: warm pale-gold light edges every vapour column and lays long reflections on the wet shelf; shadows stay cold blue-grey. Colours: arterial crimson in broad masses, ivory bone, pale teal water, wet black, small patches of orange. The mood is cold, quiet and lethal.

It must feel like a place you could walk into: firm ground in the foreground, a readable route into the middle distance, far landmarks dissolving in the air.
