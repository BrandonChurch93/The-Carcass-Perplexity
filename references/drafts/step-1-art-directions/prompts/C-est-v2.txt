A wide 16:9 landscape frame from a big-budget natural-history film about an alien world. It must read as real night-time location photography, not artwork: fast 24 mm lens at high sensitivity, available light only, true lens perspective, fine physical surface detail where light reaches it, mist thickening with distance, visible sensor noise in the shadows.

THE PLACE. Deep dusk in a shallow valley on the body of a creature the size of a mountain range that died ages ago. Its lung has weathered open here, and the valley floor is crowded with its air sacs, still alive: thousands of rounded membrane domes packed together in clusters like frogspawn, from the size of a car to the size of a house, a few much larger. Their skins are thin, leathery and wet, veined like a leaf or a bat's wing, pale where stretched and dark where slack. They breathe: at this moment some are swollen tight and others have sagged and wrinkled. Colonies of luminous organisms live inside them, so each sac glows faintly from within, cold blue-green with a dim amber core, unevenly, showing the veins and the dark shapes of things caught inside. The sacs exhale a low mist through puckered pores; it lies knee-deep over the ground, catches their light and drains slowly downhill.

VIEWPOINT AND LAYOUT, asymmetric, nothing centred. Seen from standing height on a wet black rock path. The path runs from the bottom right away down the valley between the clusters, beside a narrow black stream that mirrors the glow: the obvious route into the picture. Close on the left, one house-sized sac fills the left edge of the frame, near enough to see its veins, beads of moisture and the silhouette of a winged animal trapped inside against the light. The clusters recede down the valley at every distance, dimmer and bluer in the mist, hundreds of soft lights. Small pale moth-like animals spiral toward several of them. On the far side the valley ends at a dark cliff where one colossal ringed windpipe opening stands black against the sky. The sky is the last band of deep blue and dull orange twilight low on the horizon, with the first stars above.

LIGHT AND EXPOSURE. Exposed for the living light, so most of the frame is dark: blue-black land, soft cyan-green pools of light on mist and wet rock around each sac, a few amber points. The glow is weak, falls off quickly and is never even. The mood is silent, beautiful and lethal.

It must feel like a place you could walk into: firm ground in the foreground, a readable route into the middle distance, far landmarks dissolving in the air.
