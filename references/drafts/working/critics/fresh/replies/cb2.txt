## 2. Does a continuous zoom hold together?
Yes, mostly. Every step registers: the crater eye, the north-east lakes, the lagoon, the ribs and the orange dot all line up.

The 2811 to 2812 step is the weakest:
- The scale jump is larger than the others (about 3.5x against about 2.3x).
- The mist plume and the cliff rim disappear.
- Open sea with whitecaps turns into a calm lagoon.
- The texture changes from realistic eroded mountains to lacy, fractal relief like frosting.
It will still read as the same place, but these features visibly pop.

The 2813 to 2814 step adds a change of camera angle. The view goes from straight down to oblique, with the horizon, curvature and glare appearing, so it will look like a tilt rather than a pure rise. It won't look like a cut to a different place.

## 3. Real place or miniature?
- 2811 is the most convincing as real aerial or orbital terrain: eroded ridges, dendritic drainage, a sediment-stained crater lake and a wind plume. The slightly glossy, plaster-like look of the ridges is the only thing that suggests a relief model.
- 2812 is the weakest. The edges are over-sharp, lacy and fractal, like coral, frosting or a 3D-printed surface. The whitecaps look like sprinkled glitter or sugar, and the relief is exaggerated. It risks reading as a macro shot of an object.
- 2813 reads as large because the cloud field and open sea give scale. However, the "ribs" are regular, evenly spaced, bone-like ridges, so it reads as a giant skeleton sculpture. It is deliberately uncanny rather than a model.
- 2814 clearly reads as a view from orbit: Earth's curvature, a blue atmospheric edge, cloud fields, a hurricane at the bottom right and sun glint. The skeleton figure is plausibly continent-sized; the hurricane is about the size of a foot. Two things look small-scale: the white cloud puff at the waist looks like a cotton wad or smoke, and the red veins on the pelvis look like a stain on a small object.
