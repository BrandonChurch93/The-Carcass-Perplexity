1. It's an orbital-style view of Earth's ocean, with a giant human-like skeleton made of land, ridges and clouds lying on the surface. A hurricane sits in the lower right and the planet's curved edge shows at top left.

2. It's meant to look like an adult skeleton, but it's distorted, so it reads more as a stylized or monstrous figure than a real person. The long limbs and spread-out ribs suggest an adult, not a child. Nothing about it looks childlike: the head isn't oversized and the limbs aren't short.

3. Part by part, compared with a real adult skeleton:
- Head: about the right size, but the shape is wrong. It's a blobby, cratered landmass with one dark eye socket and doesn't look like a real skull.
- Neck: missing or far too short. The skull merges straight into the upper chest.
- Shoulders: badly placed. There's no clear collarbone or shoulder girdle, and the arms sprout from the middle of the rib area.
- Rib cage: too long and too spread out. It's split into upper and lower fans of ribs, and the lower set looks like a second rib cage.
- Waist/spine: far too long. The gap between the ribs and pelvis is huge and is filled with a cloud streak instead of lumbar vertebrae.
- Hips/pelvis: wrong shape and roughly too big. It's a pale blob with a red vein pattern and one hole, with no recognizable pelvic bowl.
- Arms: roughly right in length, maybe a bit long. The upper arm is placed oddly, and the lower arm comes off the lower ribs.
- Hands: too big, and the fingers are long, thin and spread out like rays.
- Legs: too long and thin, especially the extended right leg. The joints are vague.
- Feet: too small and stubby compared with the legs.

4. I'd shorten the torso so it's one rib cage joined to the pelvis by a short lumbar spine, removing the long gap with the cloud streak. That single fix would correct the biggest proportion error.
