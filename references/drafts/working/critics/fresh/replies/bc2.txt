Q2. Would a continuous zoom hold together?

Mostly yes: the crater and crack anchor all four as one place. But two steps are at risk.
- 5201 to 5202 is the most likely to read as a cut. The colour and light jump from dull grey-blue to warm bright, the upper-right lake turns into differently shaped dark nose pits, and the cloud wisp vanishes.
- 5203 to 5204 is the second risk. The view tilts from top-down to oblique with the planet's edge, the ribs re-form (count and spacing change) and the glowing dots disappear.
- The cloud flecks on the ocean stay roughly the same size in 5202, 5203 and 5204 even though the camera rises 2–4x. In a real ascent they would shrink, so they read as a repeated texture, not weather.

Q3. Real large place, or a miniature/model?

- 5201: the most convincing as real terrain seen from the air, with fine branching erosion ridges, a crater lake, haze and a cloud wisp. Giveaways: the crater rim is unnaturally even and sharp, the orange band is too uniform, and the crack is a thin black line cut across the ridges regardless of the terrain. It looks like a crack in plaster or dried mud.
- 5202: reads as a sculpted model or clay relief. The cross-shaped crack looks like broken ceramic, and the rounded pillar "teeth" in a regular row look moulded. The whole island being a skull points to sculpture, even though the ocean and clouds look like a real view from orbit.
- 5203: the ocean and clouds read as a view from orbit, but the land does not. The ribs are evenly spaced, parallel extruded ridges, too regular for geology, and the land looks embossed. The glowing dots read as lava or city lights, which adds a little realism.
- 5204: the planet's edge, the blue atmosphere glow, the sun glint and a hurricane at bottom right sell orbit. But a perfectly anatomical whole skeleton reads as an object laid on the sea.
- Scale cues in 5204: the hurricane is about a quarter of the skeleton's length, which implies a skeleton thousands of km long. That is consistent with orbit, but the skeleton shows no perspective squashing toward the horizon while the planet's edge curves strongly.
- Further giveaways in 5204: the cloud flecks are the same size as in the closer shots, and the lighting on the skeleton is flat.
