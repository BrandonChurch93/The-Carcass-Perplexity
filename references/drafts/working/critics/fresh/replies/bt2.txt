1. A satellite-style view of Earth's ocean (curved horizon, a cyclone at bottom right) with a huge skeleton-shaped landmass or island lying on its back, made of rocky ridges, cloud and sediment.

2. It's a skeleton-shaped geographic formation, not a real body. It reads as an adult human skeleton: an adult-sized rib cage, a long spine and long limbs. It doesn't have a child's large head-to-body ratio. Its scale is planetary, though.

3. Proportions compared with a real adult skeleton:
- Head: too big. The skull is wide and blobby, with craters for eye sockets, and roughly as wide as the rib cage.
- Neck: too short or missing. The skull merges straight into the shoulders.
- Shoulders: too narrow and poorly defined. There are no clear clavicles, and the arms come out of the top of the rib cage.
- Rib cage: about right in size but too long. The ribs are thin, widely spaced and look split into an upper and lower fan.
- Waist (lumbar spine): much too long. A thin spine with a white cloud blob runs a gap roughly as long as the rib cage, where a real one is about a third of it.
- Hips (pelvis): too small and shapeless. It's a white blob with a red mark, and there's no clear bowl or hip sockets.
- Arms: roughly right in length, but too thin. The elbow joints are vague.
- Hands: too big, with fingers much too long and spidery, like roots.
- Legs: too long and too thin, especially the outstretched one. The knees are fairly clear.
- Feet: too small. They're stubby, with short toes.

4. I'd shorten the long lumbar gap between the rib cage and the pelvis, and make the pelvis a proper, larger bowl.
