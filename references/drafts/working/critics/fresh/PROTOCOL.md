# Fresh-viewer protocol for the reveal redo (fixed before any result came in, 03:33 UTC)

What is being tested, in the user's words: "a fresh viewer names it as a giant humanoid body within two seconds and still believes it is a real continent", and for the lower frames "Nothing may read as a miniature".

Who the viewers are: newly spawned agents on separate fresh machines (`isolation: sandbox`), several different models, given one image file with a meaningless name and the brief below. They are told nothing about the film, the titan, the places or what answer is hoped for. Each viewer sees one image once and is not reused.

An agent cannot glance for two seconds. The stand-in for that is the GLANCE test: the picture shrunk to 400 px wide and one open question. The two-second read by a person is therefore not measured here; it stays UNKNOWN until a person looks.

## GLANCE brief (image at 400 px wide)
> One small image file is attached. Open it and look at it once, the way you would glance at a thumbnail in a feed. Do not zoom, enhance, research or use any tool except opening the image. Then send your answer to /root with message_agent. Answer in one sentence, the very first thing that came to mind first: what is this a picture of?

PASS: the sentence names a human or humanoid body or figure (human, humanoid, person, man, body, figure, giant, skeleton) as what the picture shows.

## FULL brief (image at full size)
> One image file is attached. Open it and look at it. Do not research anything or use any tool except opening the image. Then send your answers to /root with message_agent. Keep each answer short.
> 1. In one sentence, the first thing that came to mind: what is this a picture of?
> 2. How big is the thing shown, roughly, in real-world units, and where is the camera?
> 3. Which is it closest to, and why: (a) a landmass seen from orbit, (b) a small island or sandbar seen from an aircraft, (c) a tabletop model or sculpture, (d) a painting or drawing? Say what in the picture gives the scale away.
> 4. List every distinct feature or place you can pick out on it and where each one is.
> 5. Does anything in it look wrong, fake or small-scale? Say what.

PASS: answer 1 names a human-shaped landmass (body word plus land word), answer 3 is (a), and the size in answer 2 is continental (about a thousand kilometres or more, or the word continent or planet-scale).

Target for the reveal frame: every GLANCE viewer passes, and at least five in six FULL viewers pass. Replies are saved unedited in `critic/fresh/replies/`, with the image each one saw.

## PULL brief for the four key frames together (added and fixed 05:46 UTC, before any result came in)
These are critics, not fresh viewers: they are told that the four pictures are meant to be one rising camera move, and are asked to attack the joins.
> Four image files are attached: IMG_3391.jpg, IMG_3392.jpg, IMG_3393.jpg and IMG_3394.jpg. Open and look at all four. Do not research anything. You may crop, enlarge or overlay the images to compare them, and nothing else. Then send your answers to /root with message_agent.
> They are meant to be four moments of one camera move that rises straight up without a cut: 3391 is the lowest and 3394 the highest. Each picture should therefore be an exact closer view of one part of the next.
> 1. For each pair (3391 inside 3392, 3392 inside 3393, 3393 inside 3394): can you find the closer picture inside the wider one? Say where it sits, as percentages of the wider image's width and height, and name anything that is in one of the two pictures and is missing, moved, or differently shaped or coloured in the other. Be specific and harsh. Small differences matter.
> 2. Would a continuous zoom from 3391 out to 3394 hold together as one place, or would any step look like a cut to a different place? Say which step and why.
> 3. For each of the four pictures: does it read as a real, very large place seen from the air or from orbit, or as a miniature, a model or a small object? Say what gives that away.
> 4. In 3394, the widest: what shape is the land, and which are the most distinctive places on it?

No pass mark: every difference they name is listed in NOTES.md and checked against the pictures.
