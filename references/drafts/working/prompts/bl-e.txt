Image 1 is one part of a photograph taken from high orbit, looking almost straight down. It shows the middle of a desert continent that has the shape of a giant human body lying on its back in an ocean. At the left is the chest: long curved ridges on both sides of a black lava field that is crossed by a grey-green tidal strait, and at the far left edge the pale flat of the throat. In the middle is a shallow turquoise inland sea where the belly has sunk and flooded; a chain of small rock islands, the backbone, runs through the middle of it from left to right. Right of it lies a pale basin with dark red pools, and beyond that the two hips. Make the same photograph again: the same view from the same height. Nothing on the ground has moved.

Keep exactly as in Image 1: every coastline; every curved ridge; the black lava field with its outline, its few orange lava rivers, its small bright vents and its thin smoke; the grey-green tidal strait with its sandbars; the turquoise inland sea with its outline, its colours, its shoals and the channel that joins it to the ocean at the top; the chain of rock islands in the inland sea, every island where it is and as it is; the pale basin and the shape of its dark red pools; the mountains and plains of the hips at the right; the ocean, its shallows and its clouds at the top and bottom of the frame; the colours and the light; the edges of the frame, so that the picture still joins the pictures around it.

One thing in Image 1 is wrong. Put it right and change nothing else.

The storm. Over the inland sea Image 1 shows a single bright white cloud with a lumpy middle and long streaks flying out of it, like the puff of an explosion or the plume of an eruption. It is too white, too simple and too big in its parts, and it looks pasted onto the picture. Remove it completely, with its streaks, its violet glows and its shadow, and show the turquoise water and the islands that were under it. In its place put what a weather satellite really sees when afternoon thunderstorms build over a chain of mountainous islands, like the storms over the islands in Image 2: a loose, irregular cluster of thunderstorm tops piled over the middle part of the island chain. There are seven or eight of them and no two are alike: two large ones that have run together into one uneven mass, three of middle size, the rest small. They lie unevenly around the chain, some on it, some a little to one side: not in a row, not in a ring, not evenly spaced. Each is a soft white anvil seen from straight above: an uneven, blurred outline that thins into a grey, half-transparent veil through which the turquoise water shows, and near its middle a small, firm, brighter knot where the updraught breaks through. Thin grey veil joins some of them. Between and around them lie gaps of clear water; the islands show through in places; a few flocks of very small cumulus drift nearby. The whole cluster is about a third as wide as the inland sea, smaller than the cloud it replaces. The sun is to the right and getting low: each storm top lays a soft dark shadow on the water along its left side, and its own left side is grey, not white. In two of the storm tops a faint blush of pale violet shows through the cloud where lightning flashes underneath: small and dim, a tint only; no bolts, no rays, no bright dots.

Image 2 is a real satellite photograph of thunderstorms over a group of islands. Use it only as the standard for how such storms look from orbit: their soft edges, their unequal sizes, their thin veils and their shadows. Image 3 is a real satellite photograph of a chain of mountainous islands with cloud piled over them. Use it only as the standard for how an island chain, the cloud over it and open sea look from orbit. Do not copy the land or the sea either of them shows.

The frame is about two thousand kilometres wide; the inland sea is about four hundred kilometres across. No single cloud tower can be picked out, and nothing is seen from the side. The storm tops are flat and soft, never round balls, never cauliflower. Relief is flat to the eye: nothing is embossed, carved, sculpted or shiny.

A real photograph through the window of a spacecraft: natural colour, slightly muted by haze, sharp from edge to edge. No people, no buildings, no roads, no text, no labels, no borders. Not a sculpture, not a relief model, not an illustration.
