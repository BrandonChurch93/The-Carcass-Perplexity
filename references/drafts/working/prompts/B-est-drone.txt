A wide 16:9 landscape frame from a big-budget natural-history film about an alien world. It must read as real location photography, not artwork: a large-format digital cinema camera with a 35 mm lens on a drone about fifteen metres above the shelf, looking forward and slightly down along a tide channel, natural light only, deep focus, true lens perspective, fine physical surface detail, sea haze building with distance, slight film grain.

THE PLACE. A tidal shelf on a cold, foggy coast at low tide, early morning. The shelf is the exposed lung of a creature the size of a mountain range that died ages ago: bone-white, porous and honeycombed like weathered coral limestone, tide-stained, streaked with droppings, crusted with orange lichen and draped in black-green weed. Set into the shelf are the open ends of its airways: enormous round throats twenty to fifty metres across, each with a thick rim of ivory cartilage built from stacked rings, like the cut end of a giant ribbed hose. Most are low broken collars a few metres high; two or three are leaning stumps several storeys tall with cracked, eroded edges. All of them are exhaling at the same moment: a dense column of cold white vapour pours up out of every throat and leans the same way on the wind.

LAYOUT, asymmetric, nothing centred. From this height the nearer throats are seen partly from above as dark ringed wells with vapour pouring out of them. The nearest one is low on the right, its rim crowded with a colony of crimson feathery gill-plumes, like the crowns of giant tube worms, bent outward by the rush of air. A tide channel of pale teal seawater starts at the bottom left and winds away across the shelf between the throats toward the horizon: the obvious route into the picture. A dozen more exhaling throats stand along that channel at different distances, each smaller and paler in the fog, the farthest only grey silhouettes with their columns. In the middle distance three tall pale animals on very long thin stilt legs, with small bodies, wade the channel, tiny beside the throats. A loose flock of hundreds of small dark flyers circles one distant column. Far off on the left, almost lost in fog, the open sea and the faint curve of one colossal rib rising from the water like a mountain.

LIGHT AND WEATHER. A low white sun behind thin sea fog, back left, rims the vapour columns with light and lays long soft reflections on the wet shelf; everything else is cool, muted and damp. Colours: ivory bone, wet black rock and weed, arterial crimson plumes, pale teal water, small patches of orange lichen. The mood is cold, quiet and lethal.

It must feel like a place you could walk into: firm ground in the foreground, a readable route into the middle distance, far landmarks dissolving in the air.
