Image 1 is a photograph taken from orbit of a desert massif that has the shape of a human skull lying face up in an ocean, the crown at the left, with a neck leading to the right. Make the same photograph again: the same view from the same height. Keep everything exactly where it is and as it is: the outline of the land, both round basins, the lake and its colours, the dark nose hollow, the pale flat on the neck, the coast at the right edge, the ocean, the light. Change only the things listed here, which look too regular to be natural:

1. The teeth. The two rows of pale blocks beside the narrow channel are too even. Keep two clear rows of pale blocks, but make them worn rock: different widths and heights, a few leaning or split, two or three missing with rubble in the gaps. No two alike.
2. The channel between them, the mouth, is too straight. Give it a slight bend and let its width vary, with a sandbar or two.
3. The dry upper basin: keep it a dark, deep, round basin, as dark as it is now, but break its rim in one place with a slump of scree and a notch cut by a dry valley.
4. The nose hollow: keep it dark and the same size, but roughen its outline so it is a ragged basin, not a neat triangle.
5. The river from the lake to the mouth: remove it. Leave only a short dry gully from the gap in the lake's rim, ending in a small pale salt pan.
6. The vapour on the neck: no separate columns of smoke. One low, thin, pale haze lies over the flat and drifts toward the top of the frame as a single faint plume, half transparent, like volcanic fume seen from orbit. Keep all the dark sinkholes, with their different sizes and uneven spacing; their crimson stains become irregular streaks dragged toward the top of the frame, not neat rings.
7. The clouds over the sea: much smaller and finer. A stipple of tiny puffs in loose fields and thin streets, each with its tiny dark shadow on the water just beside it.
