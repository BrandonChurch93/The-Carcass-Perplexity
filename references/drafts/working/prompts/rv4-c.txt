Images 2 and 3 are real photographs taken from spacecraft. The picture you make must be the same kind of thing: the same real ocean and shallow banks, the same fine cloud, the same softening and blue cast from the depth of air, the same fine grain of terrain seen from very high. Do not copy what they show.

Make one photograph taken by a crew member through the window of a spacecraft in high orbit, looking almost straight down at an entire continent on an alien ocean world. Natural colour, wide 16:9 frame.

The continent is the carcass of a titan: a giant of human shape that fell on its back ages ago and has weathered into land. A viewer must see a human body at the first glance and a real continent at the second.

Scale matters most. The body is four thousand kilometres from the crown of its head to its heels and the camera is three thousand kilometres above it. One finger is a peninsula two hundred kilometres long. From this height no single mountain, hill or cloud can be picked out: mountain ranges are fine wrinkled bands with a dusting of snow, rivers are hairlines, cloud is fine white stipple, thin cloud streets and long curved frontal bands, every cloud element far smaller than a finger. Everything is slightly softened by haze, as in the real photographs.

The titan fell whole and its flesh turned to rock and soil, so the outline is a complete lean human body, not bare bones: a head, a neck, two shoulders, two arms with hands, a chest, a sunken belly, hips and two legs with feet, exactly where the map puts them. Where bone lies under the land it stands up in worn, broken mountain ranges; between and beside the ranges lie plateaus, basins, dune seas, salt pans, dark old lava plains and dry branching river systems. It is arid, bone-pale, ochre and rust, like Arabia or the Sahara from orbit. Every coast is ragged and real, with capes, bays, deltas, sand spits, lagoons, barrier islands and reefs. The fingers and toes are narrow peninsulas breaking up into chains of islets. Pale turquoise shallows of uneven width fringe the land and fall away into deep dark blue water.

The marked places, each exactly where the map shows it:
- The skull: a high pale massif in profile at the left end, face toward the top of the frame, jaw fallen open on a small bay. In its eye socket lies one perfectly round lake, deep blue at the centre with an amber ring of shallows, ringed by pale cliffs.
- The throat: a pale tidal shelf under the jaw with a line of seven dark round shafts rimmed in crimson, each trailing a thin thread of white vapour.
- The chest: the ribs show through as ten pairs of long curved pale ridges with dark valleys between them, either side of a narrow grey strait that runs down the middle of the chest.
- Beside the strait, on its upper side, a black lava field with glowing orange cracks and a thin brown fume plume drifting toward the top of the frame.
- The belly has fallen in and the sea has flooded it: a shallow turquoise sea inside the body's outline, crossed down its middle by the backbone, a single snow-white knife ridge of vertebra peaks. A tight knot of storm cloud sits on the middle of that ridge, lit from inside by violet lightning.
- The hips: two great curved ranges. In the bowl between them a salt flat stained blood red, red brine veined across white salt.

Weather and light: fine scattered cloud and cloud streets over the ocean, one large spiral storm far out at sea at the lower right and the long curved band of a weather front beyond it, thin cloud snagged on the highest ranges. Nothing hides the body. Afternoon sun from the right: relief shows mostly as colour, pale rock and snow against darker valleys, with only short fine shadows, and a broad soft patch of sunglint lies on the sea beyond the feet. The curved edge of the planet, with its thin blue line of atmosphere and black space beyond, cuts only the top left corner.

No people, no text, no labels, no borders, no spacecraft parts. Not a sculpture, not a skeleton model, not an illustration.
