Images 2 and 3 are real photographs taken from spacecraft. The picture you make must be the same kind of thing: the same real ocean and shallow banks, the same fine cloud, the same softening and blue cast from the depth of air, the same flat, fine grain of desert terrain seen from very high. Do not copy what they show.

Make one photograph taken by a crew member through the window of a spacecraft in high orbit, looking almost straight down at an entire continent on an alien ocean world. Natural colour, slightly muted by haze, wide 16:9 frame.

The continent is the carcass of a titan: a giant of human shape that fell on its back ages ago and has weathered into land. A viewer must see a human body at the first glance and a real continent at the second.

Scale matters most. The body is four thousand kilometres from the crown of its head to its heels and the camera is three thousand kilometres above it. One finger is a peninsula two hundred kilometres long. From this height the land looks almost flat: mountain ranges are fine wrinkled bands a shade paler or darker than the plains, with a thin dusting of snow only on the very highest crests; no ridge stands up, and no ridge casts a shadow you can see. Rivers are hairlines, dune seas are faint combed textures. Cloud is fine white stipple, thin streets and long curved bands, every single puff far smaller than a fingertip of the body. Nothing is carved, embossed, sculpted or shiny.

It is a body, not a skeleton. The titan fell whole and its flesh turned to rock and soil, so the land is solid and continuous from coast to coast in the outline of a complete lean human body: a head, a neck, two shoulders, two arms with hands, a chest, a waist, hips and two legs with feet, exactly where the map puts them. The trunk is one unbroken piece of land from the shoulders to the hips. Where bone lies under the land it shows as worn, broken mountain belts; between and beside them lie plateaus, basins, dune seas, salt pans, dark old lava plains and dry branching river systems. Arid, pale, ochre and rust, like Arabia or the Sahara from orbit. Nothing on it is regular or repeated: no two ridges, capes, bays or islands have the same width, length or spacing. Every coast is ragged and real, with capes, bays, deltas, sand spits, lagoons, barrier islands and reefs. The hands and feet are fans of uneven rocky capes, as the map shows them: some fingers and toes are long peninsulas, some are drowned and survive only as a stub and a chain of islets. Pale turquoise shallows of very uneven width fringe the land and fall away into deep dark blue water.

The marked places, each exactly where the map shows it, each small and fine in the frame:
- The head: a high pale massif shaped like a skull in profile at the left end, face toward the top of the frame, jaw fallen open on a small bay. In its eye socket lies one round lake a hundred kilometres across: deep blue, with a thin amber ring of shallows inside its shore and a pale cliff rim.
- The throat: a pale tidal flat under the jaw, pocked with a loose scatter of small dark sinkholes of different sizes, unevenly spaced, each with a thin crimson stain around it. A faint white haze of vapour lies over them and drifts toward the top of the frame.
- The chest: the ribs show through the land like a fold belt, about ten long curved parallel ridges on each side, uneven in width and spacing, some broken by passes, some half buried in pale sand, with darker valleys between. A narrow grey tidal strait runs between the two sides down the middle of the chest.
- Beside the strait, on its upper side, a wide matte black lava field, a dark irregular stain on the land. Across it runs a fine net of hair-thin dull orange cracks, brightest at its centre. No flames and no bright fire. One long, thin, pale brown fume plume trails from it toward the top of the frame and thins out over the sea.
- The belly has sunk and the sea has got in: a shallow turquoise inland sea, closed in by the land of both flanks, reached from the ocean by one narrow channel. Down its middle runs the backbone, a single narrow snow-white ridge. Over the middle of that ridge stands a storm: a tight cluster of many small thunderheads under one shared flat pale anvil, about three hundred kilometres across, its anvil smeared toward the top of the frame, its shadow on the sea, a faint violet tint in the cloud at its core. No drawn lightning bolt.
- The hips: two broad curved ranges. In the basin between them a salt pan: white salt crust with blood-red brine lying in pools and braided channels, dark crimson where it is deep, fading to rust and pink at the edges, like a soda lake seen from orbit.

Weather and light: cloud does not avoid the land. Thin streets of fine cloud cross one forearm and one shin, a field of fine stipple lies along one coast, a fog bank fills a bay, and thin cloud snags on the highest ranges. The head, both hands, both feet, the chest and the hips stay clear. Over the ocean, fine scattered cloud, long curved frontal bands, and one spiral storm far out at sea, partly cut off by the lower right corner of the frame, its outer bands thinning into the rest of the weather. High afternoon sun from the right: flat, even light on the land, colour doing the work, and a broad soft patch of sunglint on the sea beyond the feet. The curved edge of the planet, with its thin blue line of atmosphere and black space beyond, cuts only the top left corner.

No people, no text, no labels, no borders, no spacecraft parts. Not a sculpture, not a relief model, not a skeleton, not an illustration.
