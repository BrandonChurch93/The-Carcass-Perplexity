Image 1 is a layout, not a finished picture. It is a photograph taken from orbit in which the head and neck of a continent have been painted in as a flat sketch. The continent has the shape of a giant human body lying on its back in an ocean. This is its head, seen from straight above: a mountain massif in the shape of a human skull lying face up, the crown at the left, the chin at the right, joined by a neck to the shoulders at the right edge of the frame. Turn the sketch into a real photograph taken from orbit, about five hundred kilometres up, looking straight down.

Keep the layout exactly: the outline of the skull and the neck; the place and size of the two round eye sockets, the nose hollow, the two rows of teeth and the dark gap between them; the pale flat on the neck with its dark holes; the drift of vapour; the real coast of the shoulders at the right edge. But nothing may look drawn. Every straight edge, flat fill and clean shape in the sketch must become real terrain that erosion made: ragged coasts, broken ridges, basins, dry valleys and scree.

Images 2 and 3 are real photographs taken from orbit. Use them only as the standard for how real mountains, desert, shallows and deep water look at this height: flat, fine-grained, slightly hazy. Do not copy what they show.

The frame is about eleven hundred kilometres wide; each eye socket is about a hundred kilometres across. At this scale single hills and cliffs are too small to pick out. The land is varied, not one texture: dissected plateaus cut by hair-thin branching dry valleys, smooth pale pans, dune seas in the hollows, alluvial fans along the foot of the ranges, thin snow on the highest part of the forehead only. Relief is flat to the eye: nothing is embossed, carved, sculpted or shiny. The sun is to the right and getting low, so the light is a little warm and only the highest rims throw short soft shadows toward the left.

What is in the frame:
- The skull massif: a high, bare, pale limestone plateau, ancient and worn down. The dome of the head at the left, the face in the middle, the jaw and chin at the right. A ragged coast of cliffs, coves and narrow turquoise shallows, with only a few reefs and islands.
- The brow: a higher, paler ridge along the forehead side of both eye sockets.
- The upper eye socket is dry: a round basin with steep pale walls and a dark floor of bare rock and rubble, with tawny dune fields banked against one side.
- The lower eye socket holds a round lake. Deep, dark blue water. Just inside its shore, a narrow, uneven shelf of amber and ochre shallows, wider in some places than others; no rainbow of rings. A pale cliff rim all round, highest on the left, the brow side, where it throws a thin dark shadow on the water. At its right the rim is broken by a narrow gap, and a thread of a river runs from there past the nose toward the mouth.
- The nose: between the sockets and to their right, a dark, dry, pear-shaped hollow of shadowed rock.
- The cheekbones: under each socket a low pale ridge runs out to the coast.
- The teeth: right of the nose, two rows of blunt pale blocks of rock stand side by side like worn teeth: mesas of uneven height and width, some broken, a few missing. Between the two rows runs a narrow channel of dark sea water, the mouth, a little wider toward the bottom of the frame.
- The jaw and chin, beyond the lower teeth: lower and darker than the skull.
- The neck: low, darker desert. Along its middle lies a pale grey tidal flat with a loose scatter of dark round sinkholes of very different sizes, unevenly spaced, each a black shaft with a thin crimson stain around its mouth. Thin white vapour rises from several of them and drifts toward the top of the frame.
- Open ocean all around, deep dark blue, with fields of very fine white cloud stipple and thin cloud streets, each puff with its small shadow on the water.

A real photograph through the window of a spacecraft: natural colour, slightly muted by haze, sharp from edge to edge. No people, no buildings, no roads, no text, no labels, no borders. Not a sculpture, not a carved model, not an illustration: a real mountain massif that happens to have the shape of a skull.
