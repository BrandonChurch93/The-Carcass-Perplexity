Images 1, 2 and 3 are real photographs taken from spacecraft. The picture you make must be the same kind of thing: a real planet photographed from orbit. Image 1 is the standard for the height of the camera, the haze and blue cast of the air, the curved edge of the planet and the size of the cloud. Image 2 is the standard for bare pale rock in a dark sea: ragged capes, fine valleys with thin shadows, no relief standing up. Image 3 is the standard for stained sea water (the milky swirls of a bloom) and for a real band of frontal cloud. Do not copy what they show.

Make one photograph taken by a crew member through the window of a spacecraft in high orbit, looking almost straight down at an entire continent on an alien ocean world, late in the day. Natural colour, cold and muted, wide 16:9 frame.

The continent is a carcass: the dead body of a titan of human shape that fell on its back ages ago, rotted where it lay, dried out and weathered into land. At the first glance a viewer must see a huge dead human body, long decayed: a corpse, not somebody resting or floating. At the second glance they must see a real continent.

Scale matters most. The body is four thousand kilometres from the crown of its head to its heels and the camera is three thousand kilometres above it. One finger is a chain of capes and islands two hundred kilometres long. From this height the land looks almost flat: a mountain range is a fine wrinkled band with a thin bright edge toward the sun and a thin dark edge away from it, nothing more. Rivers are hairlines. Cloud is fine stipple, thin streets and long curved bands; every single puff is far smaller than a fingertip of the body. Nothing is carved, embossed, sculpted, polished or shiny.

What is left of the body. Not a living body with flesh on it, and not a clean white skeleton from an anatomy chart: a carcass that has lain in the open for an age.
- The bones lie bare. They are land: pale ivory-grey ranges and massifs of worn limestone, cracked, split and pitted, snow on the highest crests, scree and pale sand at their feet. Some are broken through, some have slipped out of place, some have gone under.
- Much of the hide is left, dried onto the bones like the skin of a mummy. It is land too: low, dark, leathery plateaus and plains, charcoal brown, rust and dull oxblood, shrunk tight over the bones so that every bone shows through it as a pale ridge, cracked into polygons like dried mud, holed and torn open in great ragged windows, its edges fraying into the sea. It still wraps the whole of one arm, one shoulder, one flank of the chest, one thigh and one shin: about half of the body. The skull, the other half of the rib cage, the backbone, the hips, the other arm and the other leg are bare bone.
- Where there is neither bone nor hide there is the sea. It lies between the ribs, through the belly, between the two bones of each forearm and shin, in the mouth.
- No coast is smooth. Every edge is ragged with capes, stacks, fjords, spits and chains of islets. Nothing is regular or repeated: no two ribs, fingers, capes or islands have the same width, length or spacing.

The pose is the pose of something that fell and was never moved: sprawled on its back across the whole width of the frame, head at the left, feet at the right, the trunk twisted a little at the waist. Human proportions: the body is seven and a half heads long.
- The head, at the left end: a bare skull lying face up, seen full in the face, the crown toward the left edge of the frame. Two great round eye sockets side by side, a dark triangular nose pit, cheekbones, a row of worn upper teeth. The lower jaw has dropped open and hangs askew, a horseshoe range with its own teeth, and dark sea fills the gaping mouth. In the eye socket nearer the bottom of the frame lies one round lake a hundred kilometres across: black-blue, with a thin amber ring of shallows inside its shore and a pale cliff rim. The other socket is an empty pit, dry, in shadow. A crack runs across the crown.
- The neck: a short chain of bare neck bones. Beside them, at the throat, a pale tidal shelf pierced by a loose scatter of dark round shafts of different sizes, the airways. Cold white vapour wells from them and slides low across the shelf and the water toward the top of the frame; a wine-red stain lies round the shafts.
- The shoulders: two collarbones and two shoulder knobs.
- The chest: the rib cage laid bare, about ten ribs on each side: long, thin, curved capes of pale rock with dark water or dark valleys between them, like the teeth of two broken combs facing each other. Several are snapped, two have fallen flat, and on the lower side part of the cage has caved in. The breastbone is gone: down the middle of the chest, between the tips of the two rows of ribs, runs a narrow slate-green tidal strait streaked with white tide lines.
- The heart: inside the cage, on the upper side of the strait, a matte black lava field has buried the inner ends of several ribs. At its centre a caldera holds a lake of lava: a small patch of ember orange, with a few hair-thin orange cracks running from it and one thin grey fume plume trailing toward the top of the frame. It is the only warm light in the picture.
- The belly is gone. Between the rib cage and the hips there is only dark sea, crossed by the backbone alone: one narrow range of six or seven blocky vertebra massifs, each with a short spur to each side, unequal, snow-crested, joined by low necks or parted by tidal gaps. A sheet of low cloud is dammed against the lower side of this range, and out of it, against the middle of the range, stands one thunderstorm: a dense mass of cloud with a rough top, bright on its right side, blue-grey on its left, laying a long shadow, with a faint violet glow inside it. No drawn lightning bolt.
- The hips: the pelvis, a broad pale massif shaped like a butterfly, with the two hip sockets. In its bowl lies a salt pan: white salt crust with blood-red brine in pools and braided channels, dark crimson where it is deep. Through a gap in the rim the red drains into the sea and stains it in a long rust-red plume that drifts and fades.
- The arm on the upper side is flung out and up: the upper arm bone runs from the shoulder toward the top of the frame, the elbow is bent, and the forearm, two thin parallel ranges with a slot of water between them, points back toward the head. Its hand is a claw: five thin crooked chains of capes and islets, spread.
- The arm on the lower side lies out and down from the shoulder toward the bottom of the frame. Its forearm has snapped at the middle: the far half and the hand have slipped away and lie skewed, half drowned, showing as pale shoals under the water with only the knuckles and fingertips above the surface as islets.
- The leg on the upper side is nearly straight and points to the right: a long thigh bone range, a knob of a knee, a shin of two thin parallel ranges, and a foot of long thin toe capes.
- The leg on the lower side is bent: the thigh runs out toward the lower right, the knee juts toward the bottom of the frame, and the shin doubles back to the right. Below the knee it has sunk: the shin and foot show mostly as pale drowned shoals, with a broken chain of islets along them.

The sea. Deep water is nearly black: cold slate blue. Round the carcass lies a ragged apron of pale jade-grey shoals where the body has crumbled and sunk: wide in some places, missing in others, never an even border. In the lee of the body the water is stained with slow milky swirls of sick green and ochre, like the bloom in Image 3, and with the rust-red plume from the hips.

Weather and light. Evening. The sun is low, off the right edge of the frame, and the light rakes across the body from right to left: pale bone is warm ivory on its lit sides, every hollow is a pool of shadow (the eye sockets, the nose pit, the mouth, the gaps between the ribs), and cloud lays long soft shadows on the sea. Cloud does not avoid the land: a grey frontal band curves across one forearm and one shin, fog fills two of the gaps between the ribs and one bay, thin cloud snags on the highest ranges. The skull, the rib cage, the hips, the hands and the feet stay clear enough to read. Far out at sea a spiral storm is partly cut off by the lower right corner of the frame. A broad dull band of pewter sunglint lies on the sea beyond the feet. The curved edge of the planet cuts only the top left corner of the frame, with a thin blue line of atmosphere and black space beyond it; the sea toward that corner is already dimming into dusk.

No people, no text, no labels, no borders, no spacecraft parts. Not a sculpture, not a toy, not a relief model, not an anatomy chart, not an illustration: a real photograph of a real world.
