Image 1 is one part of a photograph taken from high orbit, looking almost straight down. It shows one leg of a desert continent that has the shape of a giant human body lying on its back in an ocean. The thigh comes in from the upper left, the knee is in the middle of the frame and the shin runs on to the right edge. A range of bare mountains with a dusting of snow runs along the whole leg. At the top left lie a pale basin with dark red pools and a corner of a turquoise inland sea. At the bottom left lies one hand of the same body, with turquoise shallows between its fingers. Make the same photograph again: the same view from the same height. Nothing on the ground has moved.

Keep exactly as in Image 1: every coastline; the mountains, every ridge and the snow on them; the plains and their colours; the pale basin and the shape of its dark red pools; the turquoise shallows, the shoals and the small islands; the hand at the bottom left; the grey sheet of cloud over the sea below the thigh; the scattered cloud over the open ocean; the colours and the light; the edges of the frame, so that the picture still joins the pictures around it.

One thing in Image 1 is wrong. Put it right and change nothing else.

The cloud across the knee. From the snowy crest in the middle of the frame, down across the plain, over the coast and out over the sea toward the lower left, Image 1 shows a band of hundreds of small white dots. They are all the same size and they stand in straight, evenly spaced rows, like the holes of a perforated sheet or a printed pattern of dots. No real cloud looks like that, and the dots make the land look like a small model. Remove every one of these dots, on the land and on the sea, and show the mountains, the plain, the coast and the water that were under them, continuing the ground around them naturally. Put far less cloud in their place, of the kind a spacecraft really sees over a desert coast, as in Image 2: a few small, flat, ragged patches of white cloud, unequal in size and shape and unevenly scattered, two or three of them drawn into short broken lines that curve. Most of the plain and the coast is clear. Each patch lays a faint dark shadow on the ground just to its left. No dots, no beads, no rows, no grid, no repeated pattern anywhere.

Image 2 is a real satellite photograph of a chain of mountainous islands with cloud over them. Use it only as the standard for how small cloud over land and coast looks from orbit. Do not copy its land or its sea.

The frame is about two thousand kilometres wide; the leg is about three hundred kilometres across. The sun is to the right and getting low. No single hill, cliff or cloud tower can be picked out, and nothing is seen from the side. Relief is flat to the eye: nothing is embossed, carved, sculpted or shiny.

A real photograph through the window of a spacecraft: natural colour, slightly muted by haze, sharp from edge to edge. No people, no buildings, no roads, no text, no labels, no borders. Not a sculpture, not a relief model, not an illustration.
