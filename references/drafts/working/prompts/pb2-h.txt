Image 1 is a photograph taken from orbit of a desert massif that has the shape of a human skull lying face up in an ocean, the crown at the left, with a neck leading to the right. Make the same photograph again: the same view from the same height.

Keep the land exactly as it is, to the last detail: every coastline, both round basins, the lake and its colours, the dark nose hollow, the two rows of worn pale blocks and the bent channel between them, the pale flat on the neck with its dark sinkholes and crimson streaks, the thin haze drifting from the neck toward the top of the frame, the coast at the right edge, the turquoise shallows along every shore. Keep the colours and the light. Do not move, add or remove any landform.

Change only the open sea and the weather over it:

1. The sea is seen from far too high to show waves. Remove every white fleck and speck from the water. Open water is smooth, deep, dark blue with only faint broad mottling.

2. Add real weather as it looks from orbit over a warm sea, in three places only:
- Along the left edge and in the lower left corner: a broad field of fair-weather cumulus. Hundreds of tiny separate bright puffs, lined up in long parallel streets one or two puffs wide, the field ragged at its edge and thinning to single puffs before it reaches the land.
- Along the bottom edge, right of centre: a smaller patch of the same tiny puffs arranged as open cells, loose rings around clear holes.
- In the top left corner: a few long, thin, half-transparent streaks of high cirrus.
Every puff throws its own small dark shadow on the water, a little to the left of the puff. The shadows matter as much as the clouds.

3. At least two thirds of the sea stays clear. No cloud over the land.
