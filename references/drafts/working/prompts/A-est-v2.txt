A wide 16:9 landscape frame from a big-budget natural-history film about an alien world. It must read as real location photography, not artwork: 24 mm lens, deep focus, natural light only, true lens perspective, fine physical surface detail, humid haze building with distance, slight film grain.

THE PLACE. The inside of the windpipe of a creature the size of a mountain range that died ages ago. It is now a tunnel-cave about two hundred metres high and wide that runs straight into the distance. Its walls and roof are built from regular hoops of pale cartilage, each hoop as thick as a house, with dark wet rock between them, so the whole tunnel is ribbed, ring after ring, like the inside of a hose. Along its length the roof has fallen in at intervals, leaving ragged skylights. Through each skylight the sun comes down in one thick shaft through the wet air, and under each shaft a whole island of rainforest has grown on the rubble: tree ferns, broad-leaved giants, hanging vines, moss over the broken hoops. Thin waterfalls drop through the skylights. A river runs along the tunnel floor between the islands. The tunnel breathes: a slow tongue of cloud slides along under the roof toward the viewer and curls around the skylights.

VIEWPOINT AND LAYOUT, asymmetric, nothing centred. Seen from standing height on mossy rubble at the river's edge, in the dark stretch of tunnel before the first skylight. The river runs from the bottom left corner away down the tunnel: the obvious route into the picture. Close on the right, in shade, a fallen hoop segment covered in moss carries a cluster of pale translucent bladder-like growths, each the size of a person, a few of them puffing out fine golden dust. In the middle distance the first forest island stands in its shaft of sun, right of centre, its trees tiny under the roof, a flock of small winged animals circling up the shaft. Far beyond it, much smaller and paler in the haze, a second shaft of light falls on a second island, and beyond that a third is barely visible. The ribbed walls curve up on both sides and are lost in darkness and cloud overhead.

LIGHT AND WEATHER. The only light is daylight from the skylights: hard warm sun in the shafts, soft green bounce light near them, deep cool darkness between, wet highlights on rock and leaves, mist in every beam. Colours: deep green, wet black rock, pale bone, small amounts of gold. The mood is vast, beautiful and lethal.

It must feel like a place you could walk into: firm ground in the foreground, a readable route into the middle distance, far landmarks dissolving in the air.
