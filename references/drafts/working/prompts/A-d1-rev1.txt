CHANGES.
1. The pale bladders. In Image 1 they are too clean, like polished eggs. They are leathery fungus-like skins: matte, stained brown and green, splashed with soil, pitted and eaten in places, cracked. There are only a few rain drops on them, of different sizes, some run together. There is one small fly, not several.
2. The split bladder leaks its gold dust as a thin, uneven smoke, not a straight plume. It hugs the ground first, then is carried sideways by the draught and thins out as it rises into the light. Some of it has already settled as a dull yellow film on the nearest leaves and bones.
3. The bones are old: broken, cracked, greened with algae and half buried in the leaf litter, lying at odd angles. The jaw has lost most of its teeth; the few left are cracked, stained and uneven. Nothing is lined up.
4. The animals far off in the river, out of focus, are the heavy grazers of this place: each has a thick neck about as long as its legs and a blunt head with no ears, no trunk and no tusks. They stay soft and out of focus, standing in the river with the current breaking around their legs.

It must still read as real location photography of a place you could walk into, not artwork and not computer graphics: irregular, weathered, dirty, unposed, with fine film grain. No people, no buildings or ruins, no cameras, drones or equipment, no text, no watermark, no border.
