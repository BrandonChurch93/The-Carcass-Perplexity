Image 1 is a soft, low-resolution enlargement of one small part of a photograph taken from high orbit, looking straight down. It shows the far end of a long desert peninsula, where the land splits into five long narrow headlands spread like the fingers of a hand, with turquoise shallows around them, in a deep blue ocean. Make the same view again as a sharp photograph. It is the same place seen by the same camera; nothing has moved.

Keep exactly as in Image 1: the position, size and direction of the peninsula and of each of the five headlands; the mountain ridges and dry valleys on the peninsula; the colours of rock, sand, shallows and deep water; the light; the clouds; the edges of the frame, so that the picture still joins the pictures around it.

Change one thing. The five headlands are too smooth and too alike to be real coast: they look modelled in clay. Give them the coast that the sea cuts over ages. Each is still there and still in its place, but each has fared differently:
- Two are still whole: narrow rocky peninsulas with ragged sides, small coves, low cliffs on one side and a sandy spit on the other.
- One has been cut through by the sea in two places. Its outer half is now a line of three or four separate rocky islands with narrow dark channels between them.
- One has mostly drowned. Only its root is still land. Beyond that it runs on under shallow water as a long pale turquoise shoal, with a few small islets and reef lines standing on it.
- One is worn back to a short blunt cape, with a curved sandbar and a single island beyond it.
The gaps between them are shallow bays of uneven width with pale sand shoals, darker tidal channels and reef patches. The upland behind them is rough and dry, and its valleys drain into those bays. Nothing is symmetrical. No two tips look alike. No tip is a rounded pad.

Around the shore Image 1 has many small round brown islets, all alike, dotted evenly like pebbles. Remove them. In their place: fewer islands, cays and reef lines of very different sizes and irregular outlines, gathered where the headlands used to reach.

Images 2 and 3 are real photographs taken from orbit. Use them only as the standard for how real desert coast, islands, reefs, shoals and sandbanks look from this height: flat, fine-grained, irregular, slightly hazy. Do not copy what they show.

The frame is about a thousand kilometres wide, and each headland is more than a hundred kilometres long. No single hill, cliff, wave or building can be seen. Relief is flat to the eye: nothing is embossed, carved, sculpted or shiny. The sun is to the right and getting low. The open sea is smooth and deep blue.

A real photograph through the window of a spacecraft: natural colour, slightly muted by haze, sharp from edge to edge. No people, no buildings, no roads, no text, no labels, no borders. Not a sculpture, not a relief model, not an illustration.
