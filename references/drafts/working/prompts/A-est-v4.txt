A wide 16:9 landscape frame from a big-budget natural-history film about an alien world, shot on location. It must read as real photography of a real place, not artwork: 24 mm lens, deep focus, true lens perspective, natural light only, humid haze building with distance, slight film grain, restrained natural colour.

THE PLACE. The inside of the windpipe of a creature the size of a mountain range that died ages ago. It is now a tunnel-cave about two hundred metres high and wide that runs away into the distance, gently curving. Its walls and roof are dark wet rock crossed by great hoops of pale cartilage, each as thick as a house, so the tunnel is ribbed like the inside of a hose; but the hoops are ancient: stained, cracked, some sagging, some fallen, some half buried under flowstone, moss and hanging roots. At intervals the roof has fallen in, leaving ragged skylights. Through each skylight the sun comes down in one shaft through the wet air, and under each shaft an island of rainforest has grown on the rubble. A river runs along the tunnel floor. The tunnel breathes: a slow tongue of cloud slides along under the roof.

VIEWPOINT AND LAYOUT, asymmetric. Seen from a ledge of fallen blocks about fifteen metres above the river, in the dark stretch of tunnel well before the first skylight, so most of the frame is in cool shadow. The river runs from the bottom left away down the tunnel: the route. The first forest island is three hundred metres away, right of centre, standing in its shaft of sun: its trees are tiny under the vast roof, a mixed forest of tree ferns, pale-trunked trees with flat dark red crowns and tall bare-stemmed giants, with one thread of waterfall falling from the skylight beside it. Small flyers circle in the shaft as specks. At the river's edge near the island a herd of six-legged grazing animals drinks, tiny from here. Far beyond, much smaller and paler in the haze, a second shaft falls on a second island, and a third is only a glimmer. On the ledge in the lower right corner, in shadow, lies the old carcass of one of the grazers, sunk in moss; a patch of pale leathery puffball bladders of many sizes grows from it, a few collapsed and brown, one breathing out a thin drift of golden dust that catches a stray beam.

RESTRAINT. This is a calm, wide establishing frame. The land and the weather are the subject. Big simple shapes; wide areas of plain dark wet rock, shadow, water and haze. Life is sparse and small in the frame and sits in zones where it would really grow, never spread evenly over everything. Nothing is repeated: every hoop, tree, skylight and bladder differs from the others in size, shape, state and angle, and some are broken, some empty, some half hidden.

LIGHT AND WEATHER. The only light is daylight from the skylights: warm sun in the shafts, soft green bounce light near them, deep cool darkness between, mist in every beam, wet highlights on the river. Colours: deep green, wet black rock, pale bone, a little dark red and gold. The mood is vast, quiet and lethal.

It must feel like a place you could walk into and keep walking: firm ground at your feet, a readable route into the middle distance, far landmarks dissolving in the air, the land plainly continuing beyond every edge of the frame. Nothing posed, nothing centred, no painterly brushwork, no illustration or game-art styling. No people, no buildings or ruins, no cameras, drones or equipment, no text, no watermark, no border.
