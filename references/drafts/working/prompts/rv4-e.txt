Images 2 and 3 are real photographs taken from spacecraft. The picture you make must be the same kind of thing: the same real ocean and shallow banks, the same fine cloud, the same softening and blue cast from the depth of air, the same fine grain of terrain seen from very high. Do not copy what they show.

Make one photograph taken by a crew member through the window of a spacecraft in high orbit, looking almost straight down at an entire continent on an alien ocean world. Natural colour, slightly muted by haze, wide 16:9 frame.

The continent is the carcass of a titan: a giant of human shape that fell on its back ages ago and has weathered into land. A viewer must see a human body at the first glance and a real continent at the second.

Scale matters most. The body is four thousand kilometres from the crown of its head to its heels and the camera is three thousand kilometres above it. One finger is a peninsula two hundred kilometres long. From this height no single mountain, hill or cloud can be picked out: mountain ranges are fine wrinkled bands with a dusting of snow, rivers are hairlines, dune seas are faint combed textures, cloud is fine white stipple, thin streets and long curved bands, every cloud element far smaller than a finger. Nothing is carved, embossed or sculpted; relief is fine and shallow.

It is a body, not a skeleton. The titan fell whole and its flesh turned to rock and soil, so the land is solid and continuous from coast to coast in the outline of a complete lean human body: a head, a neck, two shoulders, two arms with hands, a chest, a sunken belly, hips and two legs with feet, exactly where the map puts them. Where bone lies under the land it stands up in worn, broken mountain belts; between and beside them lie plateaus, basins, dune seas, salt pans, dark old lava plains and dry branching river systems. Arid, pale, ochre and rust, like Arabia or the Sahara from orbit. Nothing on it is regular or repeated: no two ridges, fingers, bays or islands have the same width, length or spacing. Every coast is ragged and real, with capes, bays, deltas, sand spits, lagoons, barrier islands and reefs. The fingers and toes are uneven rocky peninsulas, some of them broken into chains of islets. Pale turquoise shallows of very uneven width fringe the land and fall away into deep dark blue water.

The marked places, each exactly where the map shows it:
- The head: a high pale massif shaped like a skull in profile at the left end, face toward the top of the frame, jaw fallen open on a small bay. In its eye socket lies one round lake a hundred kilometres across: deep blue, with a thin amber ring of shallows inside its shore and a pale cliff rim.
- The throat: a pale tidal flat under the jaw, pocked with a loose curved scatter of dark sinkholes of different sizes, each rimmed with a thin crimson stain. Thin white vapour drifts from them and merges into one faint veil blowing toward the top of the frame.
- The chest: the ribs show through the land like a fold belt, about ten long curved parallel ridges on each side, uneven in height and spacing, some broken by passes, some half buried in pale sand, with darker valleys between. A narrow grey tidal strait runs between the two sides down the middle of the chest.
- Beside the strait, on its upper side, a wide matte black lava field like a dark stain on the land, with a dull orange glow at a few vents and a long thin brown fume plume trailing toward the top of the frame and thinning out over the sea.
- The belly has fallen in and the sea has flooded it: a shallow turquoise sea inside the body's outline, crossed down its middle by the backbone, a single narrow snow-white ridge of peaks. A compact cluster of thunderheads sits on the middle of that ridge, its flat pale anvil sheared toward the top of the frame, its shadow on the sea, a faint violet flicker of lightning inside it.
- The hips: two great curved ranges. In the basin between them a salt pan: white salt crust with blood-red brine lying in pools and braided channels, dark crimson where it is deep, fading to rust and pink at the edges, like a soda lake seen from orbit.

Weather and light: cloud does not avoid the land. Thin streets of fine cloud cross one forearm and one shin, a field of fine stipple lies along one coast, a fog bank fills a bay, and thin cloud snags on the highest ranges. The head, both hands, both feet, the chest and the hips stay clear. Over the ocean, fine scattered cloud, long curved frontal bands, and one spiral storm far out at sea, partly cut off by the lower right corner of the frame, its outer bands thinning into the rest of the weather. Afternoon sun from the right: relief shows mostly as colour, pale rock and snow against darker valleys, with only short fine shadows, and a broad soft patch of sunglint lies on the sea beyond the feet. The curved edge of the planet, with its thin blue line of atmosphere and black space beyond, cuts only the top left corner.

No people, no text, no labels, no borders, no spacecraft parts. Not a sculpture, not a skeleton model, not an illustration.
