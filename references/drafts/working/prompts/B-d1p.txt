Two reference images are supplied, with different jobs.
Image 1 is a wide photograph of a real place. The new picture is taken in that same place, at the same moment, in the same light and weather. Keep its landmarks, materials, colours and atmosphere exactly consistent.
Image 2 is an unrelated real photograph. Use it only as the standard for how a real camera records light, haze and exposure. Do not copy its subject or anything in it.

Make a new 16:9 photograph: a closer shot from the same big-budget natural-history film about an alien world, taken with a 35 mm lens from a camera standing on the rock at the near lip of the large shaft in Image 1, looking along the lip and down into the shaft. It must be indistinguishable from real location photography: one plane of focus, the subject sharp, everything nearer and farther soft.

IN FOCUS. The broken lip of pale water-worn rock, and just inside it a clump of the crimson gill-plumes: soft wet feathery fronds like the gills of giant tube worms, rooted in the rock under the lip, hanging inward over the drop and lifted and stirred by cold white vapour streaming up past them, with beads of water along every frond. Below them the first cartilage hoop of the shaft wall: a thick, smooth, rounded band of ivory-white cartilage as tall as a house, wet, slightly translucent at its edge like horn, with tea-coloured drip stains, curving away round the shaft. Below it the second hoop, dimmer in the vapour, and then darkness. The vapour wells up, spills over the lip at one low place and slides away across the rock at ankle height. In the shallow hollow where it pools lies one of the white wading animals, dead, on its side with its stilt legs folded and its long neck stretched out, and beside it the clean bones of two older ones.

BEHIND, SOFT BUT RECOGNISABLE AS IMAGE 1. The far lip of the shaft; the pale shelf and the tide channel with sun glitter; a few living waders as small pale shapes; the far vapour column; the cliff with its wide cave and the fog pouring from it.

WHAT MAKES IT REAL. The same single light source and weather as Image 1. Real materials with weight and wetness, small flaws, dirt and asymmetry; nothing carved, patterned, glossy or symmetrical; nothing repeated. The subject is caught as found, not arranged for the camera, and part of it is cut off by the edge of the frame. Natural exposure with gentle highlights and open shadows, no added sharpening, no glow added in post, restrained colour, fine film grain.

No people, no buildings, no equipment, no text, no watermark, no border.
