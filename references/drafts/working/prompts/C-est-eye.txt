It must read as real location photography, not artwork: a large-format digital cinema camera with a fast 24 mm lens at high sensitivity, standing height on a wet ledge, available light only, true lens perspective, fine physical surface detail, mist thickening with distance, slight film grain.

THE PLACE. The last deep-blue light after dusk, inside the collapsed windpipe of a creature the size of a mountain range that died ages ago. It is now a gorge two hundred metres wide and open to the sky. Both walls are built from evenly spaced curved ribs of pale cartilage, ring after ring receding into the distance like the inside of a giant ribbed hose with its roof torn away; black moss and dripping water cover them. A slow river of fog fills the gorge floor and slides along it like breath. From the underside of every ring hang curtains of thousands of fine vertical threads strung with beads of sticky fluid, shining cold blue-green like glow-worm snares. Drifting through the air at several depths are large translucent bell-shaped animals trailing long thin tentacles, each lit faintly from inside, pale cyan with a small amber core. These living lights are the only light sources apart from the sky: they light the fog and the wet cartilage close to them and leave the rest in near darkness.

LAYOUT, asymmetric, nothing centred. The camera stands on a broad wet ledge on the left wall, a third of the way up, looking along the length of the gorge. The ledge continues ahead as a walkable shelf along the wall: the obvious route into the picture. Close on the right, one curtain of glowing threads hangs just beyond arm's reach, a small winged animal caught and wrapped in it. One floating bell-animal, about ten metres across, passes in the middle distance at eye level over the fog; four or five more are small points of light at increasing distances. Far away the gorge forks into two narrower branches. Above the walls is a strip of dark blue sky with a few stars and a band of last light near the horizon at the far end.

LIGHT AND EXPOSURE. Exposed for the living lights, so most of the frame is dark: deep blue-black shadows, soft cyan and green pools of light on fog and wet surfaces, a few warm amber points. The glow falls off quickly and realistically; nothing is evenly lit. The mood is silent, beautiful and lethal.
