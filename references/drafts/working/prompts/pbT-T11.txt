Images 2 and 3 are real photographs taken from orbit. Use them only as the standard for how real land, shallows, deep water and cloud look at this height: flat, fine-grained, slightly hazy. Do not copy what they show.

The frame is about two thousand kilometres wide. At this scale no single hill, cliff, wave or cloud puff can be picked out. Mountain belts are fine wrinkled bands a shade paler or darker than the plains, cut by hair-thin branching dry valleys; plains carry faint dune fields, pale pans and alluvial fans; snow is a thin dusting on the highest crests only. Relief is flat to the eye: nothing is embossed, carved, sculpted or shiny, and no ridge casts a shadow you can see. Coasts are ragged and real, with coves, spits, lagoons, reefs and small islands, and turquoise shallows of uneven width that fall away into deep dark blue water. Cloud over the sea is very fine white stipple, thin streets and long soft bands, each puff tiny, with a pin-prick shadow on the water. The sun is to the right.

- At the left, the chest of the continent: its ribs show through the land as long curved parallel ridges of pale rock with darker sandy valleys between, on both sides of a narrow grey sea strait. At the far left edge, the end of a matte black lava field with hair-thin dull orange cracks.
- In the middle, the belly: a shallow turquoise inland sea, closed in by land on both sides and reached from the ocean by one narrow channel at the top. Sandbanks and pale shoals show through the water.
- Down the middle of that sea runs the backbone: a single narrow ridge of snow-white peaks.
- Over the ridge stands a storm. Make it a real storm system seen from orbit: not one cloud but a tight cluster of many small thunderheads, each a tiny bright knot, under one shared thin pale veil of anvil cloud that is smeared toward the top of the frame. Its shadow lies on the sea beside it. A faint violet tint at its core. No drawn lightning bolt.
- Right of the sea, the hips. In the basin between two curved ranges lies a salt pan: white and pale pink salt crust with blood-red brine in hair-thin braided channels and many small pools, dark crimson where it is deepest, fading to rust and pink at the edges, like a soda lake seen from orbit.
