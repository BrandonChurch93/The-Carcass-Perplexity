A wide 16:9 landscape frame from a big-budget natural-history film about an alien world, shot on location. It must read as real dusk photography of a real place, not artwork: fast 24 mm lens at high sensitivity, available light only, true lens perspective, natural light only, thin mist thickening with distance, visible sensor noise in the shadows, restrained natural colour.

THE PLACE. Late dusk in a long shallow valley on the body of a creature the size of a mountain range that died ages ago. Its lung has weathered open here. The valley floor is bare wet black rock, and along the stream and the seeps grow its air sacs, still alive: soft membrane bladders in bunches like frogspawn or grapes, a few to a few dozen in a bunch, from the size of a barrel to the size of a house, joined by ropes of wet tissue. They sag, wrinkle and lean on each other; some are swollen tight, some have collapsed into folds, many are dark and dead. Luminous organisms live inside the living ones, so they glow faintly and unevenly from within, cold blue-green, a few with a dim amber core, and each lit bunch throws a soft pool of light onto the mist and the wet rock around it. They exhale a thin mist that gathers in the hollows and drains downhill in slow tongues.

VIEWPOINT AND LAYOUT, asymmetric. Seen from a rise of rock about eight metres above the valley floor. A narrow black stream runs from the bottom right away down the valley, mirroring the lights: the route. In the lower left corner, close, one small bunch of three sacs on the rock, one of them collapsed, the largest showing its veins and the dark shape of something caught inside. Down the valley the bunches follow the stream at every distance, with bare dark ridges of rock between them, dimmer and bluer with distance, until the valley bends left behind a spur of cliff, where a wider glow on the mist shows that the field goes on out of sight. Halfway down, small against a lit bunch, the dark shape of a large six-legged animal walks toward the light. Swarms of tiny pale flyers hang around the nearest lights only. The far side of the valley is a dark cliff with ragged cave mouths that breathe out mist. Above the ridge the sky is deep blue-grey dusk under thin cloud, with a faint band of dull afterglow low on the horizon and no stars.

RESTRAINT. This is a calm, wide establishing frame. The land and the weather are the subject. Big simple shapes; wide areas of plain dark wet rock, mist and sky. Life is sparse and small in the frame and sits in zones where it would really grow, never spread evenly over everything. Nothing is repeated: every bunch and sac differs from the others in size, shape, state and angle, and some are broken, some empty, some half hidden.

LIGHT AND EXPOSURE. Exposed for the living light, so most of the frame is dark and quiet: blue-black land, soft cyan-green pools of light with real fall-off on mist, rock and water, a few amber points, the last cold skylight on the wet rock. The mood is silent, beautiful and lethal.

It must feel like a place you could walk into and keep walking: firm ground at your feet, a readable route into the middle distance, far landmarks dissolving in the air, the land plainly continuing beyond every edge of the frame. Nothing posed, nothing centred, no painterly brushwork, no illustration or game-art styling. No people, no buildings or ruins, no cameras, drones or equipment, no text, no watermark, no border.
