A wide 16:9 landscape frame from a big-budget natural-history film about an alien world. It must read as real location photography, not artwork: a large-format digital cinema camera with a 35 mm lens on a drone about ten metres above a stream, flying up a gorge beneath the canopy, natural light only, deep focus, true lens perspective, fine physical surface detail, humid haze building with distance, slight film grain.

THE PLACE. A warm, dripping cloud-forest gorge that has grown inside the collapsed lung of a creature the size of a mountain range that died ages ago. What stand here in place of trees are its airways: colossal hollow branching tubes of pale bone-white cartilage, ringed along their whole length like windpipes, forking again and again as they rise, the thickest wider than a house. Moss, ferns, hanging roots and epiphytes cover them. Instead of leaves their finest branches carry heavy clusters of translucent amber air sacs, each sac the size of a small car, thin-skinned and veined like a bladder, lit up where sun passes through them. Some sacs are venting: slow clouds of fine golden spore dust hang in the shafts of light.

LAYOUT, asymmetric, nothing centred. The shallow clear stream runs below, from the bottom right away up the gorge over mossy boulders: the obvious route into the picture. Low on the left, a fallen ringed airway lies half sunk in moss, its open hollow end visible. Ahead, one enormous ringed airway arches right over the stream like a natural bridge, sac clusters hanging beneath it, backlit; the camera is about to fly under it. Under and beyond the arch the gorge opens into a brighter, misty clearing where more branching airways stand at different distances, each paler in the haze, and a thin waterfall falls from a honeycombed cliff far at the back. On the stream bank below lies the carcass of a large six-legged grazing animal, furred over with moss and sprouting pale fruiting stalks. Small winged animals circle in the light at several distances.

LIGHT AND WEATHER. Low late-afternoon sun entering from the back right through gaps above, in separate shafts through wet air; warm gold in the light, deep green-black in the shade, every surface wet. Colours: deep moss green, pale bone, amber and gold. The mood is beautiful, close and lethal.

It must feel like a place you could walk into: firm ground in the foreground, a readable route into the middle distance, far landmarks dissolving in the air.
