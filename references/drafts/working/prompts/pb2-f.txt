Image 1 is a photograph taken from orbit of a desert massif that has the shape of a human skull lying face up in an ocean, the crown at the left, with a neck leading to the right. Make the same photograph again: the same view from the same height. Keep everything exactly where it is and as it is: the outline of the land, both round basins, the lake and its colours, the dark nose hollow, the pale flat on the neck, the coast at the right edge, the ocean, the light. Change only the things listed here, which look too regular to be natural:

1. The teeth. The two rows of pale blocks beside the narrow channel are too even. Make them worn, broken rock: blocks of very different widths and heights, some leaning, some split, several missing with rubble and sand in the gaps, a few fallen into the channel as islets. They must still read as two rows of teeth, but no two alike.
2. The channel between them, the mouth, is too straight. Let it bend and change width: pinched almost shut by sandbars in one place, wider in another, with pale tidal flats along its edges.
3. The dry upper basin is too perfect a circle. Break its rim: one side slumped into scree, a notch cut by a dry valley, dunes spilling through. The lake below it stays round.
4. The nose hollow: roughen its outline into a ragged, irregular basin with scree fans on its floor.
5. The river from the lake to the mouth: remove it. Leave only a short dry gully from the gap in the lake's rim, ending in a small pale salt pan.
6. The vapour on the neck: no separate columns of smoke. One low, thin, pale haze lies over the flat and drifts toward the top of the frame as a single faint plume, half transparent, like volcanic fume seen from orbit. The dark sinkholes stay, with different sizes and uneven spacing; their crimson stains become irregular streaks dragged toward the top of the frame, not neat rings.
7. The clouds over the sea: much smaller and finer. A stipple of tiny puffs in loose fields and thin streets, each with its tiny dark shadow on the water just beside it.
