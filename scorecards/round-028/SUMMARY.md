## Severe issues, word for word

1. `R4-1`, set-A-04.png, the climb from the lake to the overhead coast, frames 9337–9841: "The lake-level composition gives way to an overhead ocean view without a recognizable shoreline carrying the eye through the climb. After the climb the clouds and ocean highlights read as attached to the planet image rather than moving at their own heights". Asked: "Carry a recognizable bend in the shore through the pitch and climb, then let the cloud layers move independently over the surface".
2. `R4-1`, set-A-03.png, the large animal and juvenile in the red shallows, frames 6309 and 6561: "The camera gets closer over four seconds, but the animals keep nearly the same stance and spacing, with little sense that the adults or young are loading or releasing their feet in the mud". Asked: "Give the adult a visible weight transfer and a planted step, let the juvenile pause or turn independently, and disturb the shallows at their feet".
3. `R5`, set-A-07.png, frame 10027, the last wide view of the planet: "none of the seven places can be found in the skeleton-continent. The arches, the lava field, the terraced mudflat, the red marsh, the ice ridge and the crater lake have no matching landmark in it. Only a red star at the chest and a white plume might stand for the lava and the fume. So the reveal says 'it was a body' but not 'it was these places'". Asked: "put recognisable landmarks from each place on the body at their real positions: rib arches along the ribcage, the lava glow where the heart is, the crater lake as an eye socket, red marsh pools along the gut, ice on the high limb. Let the camera's pull-out pass over one or two of them so they can be followed".
4. `R6`, set-A-01.png to set-A-10.png, across the entire frame: "almost nothing changes across the opening sequence beyond a very slow push-in, rolling waves, and drifting mist, so with sound off it reads like a static establishing plate rather than a hook that rewards staying". Asked: "introduce an immediate, unmistakable visual change in the opening frames such as a faster push through the arch, a subject emerging from the mist, or a dramatic lighting shift".
5. `R7-2`, set-B-05.png, the lower-left fractured rock slope and recessed grey rock face in the top crop, also visible at full frame in set-B-01-b.png: "compression smears the crisp rock striations and fine stone texture into heavy, muddy, blurry blotches that stand out even at full-frame delivery size". Asked: "raise bitrate and reduce aggressive deblocking or temporal smoothing so textured foreground rock retains sharp detail without smearing".
6. `R7-2`, set-B-03.png, the dark overhanging rock ledge and adjacent cliff face in the bottom crop: "the dark rock overhang and surrounding ridges smear into blurry, blotchy patches with severe loss of surface texture and edge definition". Asked: "allocate more bits to dark textured foregrounds and soften encoder filtering so rock ridges do not turn to mush".
7. `R7-2`, set-B-02.png, the upper-left dark gnarled growth cluster clinging to the cliff in the top crop, and the lower clusters in set-B-04.png and set-B-05.png: "fine organic surface detail and jagged silhouettes collapse into mushy, smeared blobs with dark halos that will crawl during playback". Asked: "increase bitrate and use psychovisual rate control tuned to preserve complex dark foreground detail".

## Major issues, word for word

1. `R4-1`, set-A-06.png, the lava cascade below the opening, frames 4215–4220: "Bright threads change inside an almost fixed outline, giving the cascade the look of an animated glow rather than a thick stream turning over its own skin". 2. `R4-1`, set-A-09.png, the rear feet of the animal at the cave mouth, frames 8299–8304: "Both feet remain almost equally separated from the snow, with soft indistinct contact, so the animal reads as suspended before the cut". Asked: "Set one foot firmly against the slope, transfer the body weight over it, and let the other foot release with a small snow scuff".
3. `R4-2`, set-B-03.png, the pale shore animals, especially the nearest one on the right, from loop frames 44 to 305: "the bodies shift along the shore, but the long legs retain much the same spread and no planted foot is easy to pick out; the animals read as sliding more than stepping". Asked: "Hold each planted foot in place while the body moves over it, then stagger the lifts, pauses, and turns across the group".
4. `R4-2`, set-B-01.png, the low fog bank to the right of the front rib, frames 0 to 120: "the foreground sea changes shape, but the fog keeps the same broad diagonal streaks as the framing creeps forward, so it reads more like a fixed layer than heavy air flowing around the ribs". Asked: "Let the bank pool against the rib bases, peel off the far sides, and break into low eddies at a different pace from the camera move".
5. `R4-2`, set-B-02.png, the right-hand spiral and the wisps over the ribs in the orbital hold, frames 11623 to 11743: "the glow dims and the view drifts, but the spiral and nearby wisps keep almost the same outlines and stay pinned to the ground markings, making the hold read like a dimming still". Asked: "Give the high cloud and storm bands subtle, independent drift against the terrain without speeding up the orbital move".
6. `R5`, set-A-07.png, frame 10027, the skeleton against the ocean: "the bones read as a flat decal on top of the ocean. Wave texture and whitecaps run straight through the land, there is no coastline, relief or shadow at the edge, and the hurricane is pasted over the shoulder. It looks like an illustration laid on a globe, not terrain". Asked: "model the body as land with a real shoreline, surf and relief shadows. Mask the ocean texture out of the landmass and make the clouds and storm sit above the terrain in depth".
7. `R5`, set-A-06.png, frame 7015, the ice ridge with lightning: "this place looks less finished than the others. The cloud sea is soft and lumpy like CG, the snow looks plastic, the lightning bolt is a bright vector stroke that lights nothing around it, and the far spires are cardboard cut-outs against an orange band". Asked: "bring it up to the photoreal level of the crater lake. Give the lightning a scatter glow and a flash on the ridge, and add volume, detail and atmospheric depth to the cloud sea and the far peaks".
8. `R5`, set-A-06.png, frame 9541, the transition between the crater lake and orbit: "the frame is a featureless grey-blue blur. Paused, it looks like a render failure, not a move through cloud". 9. `R6`, set-A-01.png, the dark curving spires in the left half: "at phone size it is unclear whether the shapes are rock sea stacks, giant horns, or creature limbs, and there is no scale cue to immediately ground what the viewer is looking at". 10. `R6`, set-A-01.png to set-A-10.png, the right half of the frame and overall palette: "roughly the right 40 percent of the frame is empty grey mist and clouds, and the muted grey-on-grey palette lacks a focal highlight or color contrast to stop a fast-scrolling viewer on a phone". 11. `R6`, set-A-06.png to set-A-10.png, the touching tips of the main arch and the smaller inner tendril: "both the main spires and the smaller inner spike meet at tiny, awkward pinch-point tangents that feel accidental once noticed".
