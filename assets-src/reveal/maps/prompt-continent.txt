An orthographic satellite image of a whole continent on an ocean world, looking straight down, with no cloud anywhere, no haze, no atmosphere edge and no sun glare on the sea: a cloud-free composite as a mapping satellite makes it, in soft light from the upper left that gives the mountain ranges gentle relief shading.

The first reference image is the continent. Keep its framing, and keep every mountain range exactly where it lies and at the same size: the ranges are the bones of a dead giant lying on its back, head at the left, feet at the right. Remove the cloud, the white storm on the backbone, the cyclone at the lower right, the bright glare at the upper right, and the planet's edge and black sky at the upper left: where they were there is open dark blue-black sea.

Change one thing. This is a real continent first, with more land around the bones. The bones are its high mountain ranges: pale ivory-grey rock, with snow on the highest crests and fine branching drainage running down both flanks. Around and between the ranges lies lower country in darker values than the bones, so that the pale ranges still stand out at once: dark basalt plains, grey-brown badlands cut by canyons, dull olive-grey steppe, ochre desert basins, braided rivers running to the coast, deltas, and a ragged coastline with bays, capes, offshore islands and wide teal shelves of shallow water. The land is about twice as wide as the bones alone, and no two regions look alike. The sea still fills the long channel down the middle of the chest between the two rows of rib ranges, the open belly between the rib cage and the hips, where the backbone crosses the water as one thin ridge, the gap between each arm and the trunk, and the gap between the legs. The skull keeps the round black-blue lake in its eye socket with a thin amber rim. The hip basin keeps its white salt flat with dark red channels. The chest keeps a small dull red glow of lava. Fingers, toes and teeth are not drawn exactly: they are broken chains of islands and ragged spurs.

The second reference image is a real satellite photograph: it is the standard for how a satellite records real land and sea, with real colour, real erosion at every scale, and no painted, stylised or generated look.

Seen small, the picture must still read at once as the skeleton of a giant lying across the sea. Photographic, sharp from edge to edge. No text, no labels, no grid lines, no border, no logo, no stars.
