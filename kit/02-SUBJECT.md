# 02 · The world, the six places and the reveal

## 1 · The rule of this world

A titan died here ages ago. Its body is the land. The anatomy lies bare as geology at the scale of a continent, worn by sea and weather. Life is sparse and strange and gathers only where the body still leaks something: vapour, heat, blood, brine. Each place has one colour of its own, one weather and its own animals, so no two places could be mistaken for each other. The thing that kills you in each place is the place itself, plain physics, and it is always in frame as a line you should not cross.

Nothing in the film says the land is a body until the camera rises. Up close every place is a real landscape first. The ribs are sea stacks, the heart is a caldera, the eye is a lake. A viewer may half notice. They are not told.

This direction was chosen from three by blind critics and approved by Brandon. The record of how it was found is `references/drafts/ART-DIRECTIONS.md` and `references/drafts/NOTES.md`. Read the section of `NOTES.md` on a place before you build it: it holds the prompt, the real photographs used as a rendering standard and the known faults of every approved picture.

## 2 · What is true of every place

- It is a place you could walk through, with ground under the camera, distance behind the subject and weather between them.
- The film opens it on the establishing view, then the camera travels and pushes in on details. Each place has a top-down layout sketch in `references/approved/` that shows where its four details sit and one route between them. The route is a suggestion. The distances on the sketches are design intent, not measurements.
- Everything that could move does. Each place lists what moves. That list is a floor, not a ceiling.
- The animals belong to their place and to no other. Nothing living has eyes or ears: where a head would be there are tendrils, plumes, feelers or nothing. They are never cute and never monsters. They are animals getting on with it.
- In every place something dead is in frame, killed by the place. It is shown plainly and never dwelt on.
- No place repeats another's colour or weather. If two of your places start to look alike, the one further from its approved pictures is wrong.

## 3 · The six places

The keys of each place are in `keys` in the config, with the path of each approved picture.

### The Ribs · `ribs` · with the opening

- The place: a tidal strait between two rows of rib blades that stand out of the sea and lean in overhead, hundreds of metres high. Low tide, sea wind, the sky clearing after a storm. Black weed covers every rib up to the tide line, about thirty metres up. Above it the rock is pale and streaked white by the colony.
- Its colour and weather: slate-green sea, white surf, pale stone, rust lichen, black weed. Wind and broken cloud.
- What lives there: kite-wings, pale long-legged fliers with a tuft of tendrils for a head, wading the pools at low tide and nesting on the ledges above the weed line. Small pale crabs. Worms in the pools.
- The line: the weed line. The tide comes back through the cage as one wall of water, and below that line nothing that cannot fly is alive when it arrives.
- The details: the drowned animal hanging in the weed on the near rib (`ribs-1`), the tide-pool feeders (`ribs-2`), the colony on its ledges (`ribs-3`), the bore arriving between the ribs (`ribs-4`).
- What moves: surf and spray, water running off the weed, runnels draining across the sand, kite-wings feeding, landing and lifting off, cloud moving, the bore.
- The opening belongs to this unit. It has no approved picture of its own. The viewer comes in from the open sea, low over the water, in the weather the Ribs pictures show, and the first blades come out of it far larger than they first seemed. It has to stop a scroll before a word is spoken.
- Known weak points of the pictures, which the build may improve on and must not make worse: the establishing view is the most composed of the six and can read as a poster, and the bore in `ribs-4` looks more like storm surf than one wall of water. The words say the sea returns all at once. Make it one wall.
- Layout: `references/approved/1-ribs/ribs-layout.png`. The strait floor is about 600 m wide.

### The Heart · `heart` · the look lock

- The place: a caldera lake of lava about a kilometre across under walls three hundred metres high, at nightfall. The lake is covered by a grey-black crust broken into plates, with glowing seams between them. A vessel mouth in the far wall pours a lava fall into the lake. On the near side a bench of old solid lava runs along the shore, with one steaming sulphur crack in it.
- Its colour and weather: black, ember orange, sulphur yellow. Fume, heat shimmer, a dim sky.
- What lives there: crust-walkers, tall animals on thin legs that walk the plates and feed at the seams through a trunk that frays into tendrils.
- The line: where solid rock ends and the crust begins. The crust looks like ground. A plate founders and the lake takes whatever stood on it.
- The details: the lava surface at the line (`heart-1`), the one that sank, half under the folded skin of the lava with two legs still arched out of it (`heart-2`), the walkers feeding at a bright seam (`heart-3`), the vessel mouth and its lava fall (`heart-4`).
- What moves: seams opening and closing, plates drifting and foundering, spatter, the lava fall, fume, heat shimmer, sulphur steam, walkers stepping. The whole lake breathes: the glow of the seams rises and falls slowly, like a pulse.
- Known weak point: the distant walkers all share one silhouette. Vary them.
- Layout: `references/approved/2-heart/heart-layout.png`.

### The Breath · `breath`

- The place: a tidal shelf of pale bone-rock planed flat by the sea, at a cold dawn with sea fog and a low sun. Shafts open in the shelf: the airways. The largest is about sixty metres across, ringed inside with ruined cartilage hoops, with one collapsed side. A tide channel crosses the shelf. A sea cliff with a cave stands three kilometres on, with fog pouring out of it down the scree.
- Its colour and weather: ivory rock, teal water, wine-red plumes, warm low sun through cold fog.
- What lives there: waders, tall pale four-legged animals with plumed tendrils for a head, feeding in the tide channel. Gill-plumes, crimson feathered growths rooted on the lip of the shaft where the vapour leaves it. These are the red weed of the narration.
- The line: the rim. The air that slides out of the shafts is heavy and has nothing in it to breathe. It lies low over the rock and pools at the rim. One wader lies dead there and another stands over the drop.
- The details: waders feeding in the channel (`breath-1`), the rim with the dead one (`breath-2`), the gill-plumes on the lip (`breath-3`), down the shaft (`breath-4`).
- What moves: vapour sliding out of the shaft and pooling, the fog bank, water moving in the channel, waders feeding, plumes swaying and dripping.
- This was the hardest place to design, because its subject is air. The vapour has to read as a heavy thing with weight and a surface, not as haze.
- Layout: `references/approved/3-breath/breath-layout.png`.

### The Blood Marsh · `marsh`

- The place: salt flats under a white overcast, with no wind and rain hanging far off. The old blood still seeps up: pools and branching channels of red brine with white salt rims. The main channel, the vein, is about ten metres wide. The largest seep pool steams and bubbles. An escarpment stands six kilometres on.
- Its colour and weather: oxblood water, white salt, crimson fans, flat white light.
- What lives there: stalk colonies, salt-crusted stalks with crimson fans, growing round the seep pools with small winged insects round them. Mud-wallowers, heavy eyeless animals with tusk-like feelers, rooting in the channels.
- The line: the edge of the channel. Between the channels a thin salt crust lies over deep mud, and the mud holds what steps on it. The dead are still there, crusted with salt, and one is still alive.
- The details: the bubbling seep pool (`marsh-1`), the stalk colony (`marsh-2`), the mud-wallowers (`marsh-3`), the one on the thin crust (`marsh-4`).
- What moves: gas domes rising and bursting, rings spreading on the pools, steam, insects, wallowers rooting, fans dripping, the rain curtain far off.
- Known weak points: the wallowers sit close to a walrus and the stalks read as palms. Push both further from anything on Earth.
- Layout: `references/approved/4-marsh/marsh-layout.png`.

### The Spine · `spine`

- The place: a knife ridge of vertebrae above a sea of cloud, at dusk, with a storm standing against one side of it and a gale blowing across. Each vertebra is a mountain with a leaning spire, and the tip of every spire is burned black. A canal runs through the whole ridge from one vertebra to the next. The cloud sea lies about two thousand metres below.
- Its colour and weather: blue-grey rock, snow, violet lightning, a last band of orange at the horizon. Gale, spindrift, hail.
- What lives there: tether-kites, translucent ribbons that stream downwind from stalks anchored on the lee face of each spire. Ridge-runners, shaggy animals with tendrils for a face, which cross the snow saddles and shelter inside the canal.
- The line: the crest. The lightning walks the ridge from spire to spire. Whatever is on the crest when it comes is where it comes to ground.
- The details: the scorched ridge-runner where a bolt came to ground (`spine-1`), the tether-kites on the first spire (`spine-2`), the strike on the third spire, seen with a long lens (`spine-3`), inside the canal where the herd is sheltering (`spine-4`).
- What moves: storm cloud pouring over the low saddles, lightning, spindrift and hail, kites streaming, the cloud sea drifting, the herd shifting.
- Known weak points: this is the place closest to a poster, and the ridge-runners read as dogs. The shaggy coat is also the hardest surface in the film to build. `kit/03-METHOD.md` section 4 says what to do if it will not hold up close.
- Layout: `references/approved/5-spine/spine-layout.png`. The spires stand about 800 m apart.

### The Eye · `eye`

- The place: a round lake about two kilometres across in a socket of cliffs two hundred metres high, on a clear, still evening. A ring of amber shallows, knee-deep and clear, surrounds a centre that goes all the way down: a shaft with sheer walls and no bottom in sight. On one side a brow of cliff overhangs the water. A stream leaves through a gap in the rim.
- Its colour and weather: black-blue water and amber, warm low light on pale cliffs. No wind at all.
- What lives there: drinkers, pale eyeless long-necked animals that come down to the shore in a herd and never go deeper than their ankles. Amber filament mats on the floor of the shallows, all combed toward the centre. Pale forms hanging in the deep.
- The line: the edge of the shallows. The floor stops without warning, and something lives in the centre.
- The details: drinkers at the shore (`eye-1`), the amber mats (`eye-2`), the edge where the floor stops, with a skeleton on it (`eye-3`), what rises from the centre (`eye-4`).
- What moves: rings spreading on the water, filaments swaying toward the centre, threads of bubbles, mist at the foot of the cliffs, drinkers, the water doming at the centre. This is the quietest place in the film and should feel like held breath, not like a still picture.
- What rises is never shown whole. A dome of water, a pale shape under it, and the camera is already leaving.
- Known weak points: the drinkers are the least alien animals in the set. They may be pushed further.
- Layout: `references/approved/6-eye/eye-layout.png`.

## 4 · The reveal · `reveal`

- The shot: the camera leaves the surface of the lake in the Eye and rises until the whole continent is in frame, with no cut. The continent is the titan: a dead giant on its back, head at the left, feet at the right, weathered down to bone-pale mountain ranges in a dark sea. The curve of the planet shows only at one edge of the widest frame.
- Brandon rejected an earlier reveal because it read as a fish with arms growing out of its head, and then asked for this one to look more decomposed and creepier. The rule that came out of that: the outline says corpse, the surface says continent. The outline is a body that has come apart, with the jaw fallen open, the breastbone gone, the belly gone and one knee bent. The surface is geology: every bone is a mountain range with drainage, every hollow is sea, shallows, salt or lava. Keep both halves of that rule. Do not tidy the body and do not add flesh to it.
- How a body on its back shows both its ribs and its spine: the breastbone has rotted away, so the sea runs down the middle of the chest between the two rows of rib ranges. That channel is the strait of the Ribs. Below the last ribs there is no belly wall left, and the backbone crosses open water from the rib cage to the hips as one knife ridge. That is the Spine, and the storm sits on it.
- The four keys, `reveal-1` to `reveal-4`, are four heights of one rising camera. All four are views of one picture, `references/approved/7-reveal/reveal-master.jpg`. `references/approved/7-reveal/pullback.json` gives the exact window of each key in that master, how each nests in the next, and where the six places sit on the body. `reveal-marked.jpg` shows those six marks.
- The aim of the camera is not locked to the lake. The lake is at the centre of the first two keys and then moves left, so the aim eases from the lake toward the chest while the camera rises, and the body ends up filling the frame.
- The six places must be findable from orbit and look like themselves: the strait between the rib ranges, the lava glow in the chest, the airways in the throat with their vapour, the red salt flats in the hips, the storm on the backbone, the round lake in the eye socket. The viewer has just been to all six. Seeing them again from above, in their own colours, is what makes the reveal land.
- What moves on the way up and at the top: cloud drifting at its own height and its shadow on the sea, the storm on the backbone flickering from inside, the lava glow pulsing, vapour trailing from the throat, sunglint sliding on the water, the planet's edge arriving.
- The scale does not agree between the places and the reveal, and it does not have to. The Eye is drawn as a lake two kilometres across. The first key of the reveal shows the same lake about a hundred kilometres across. The film never shows both at once. The handoff happens early in the rise, and it must be invisible: weather, speed and height hide it, and the viewer feels one climb.
- Known weak points of the keys, named by fresh viewers in the reference run and listed in full in `NOTES.md`: the lower frames read as smaller places than intended and the second key can read as a close-up of an object, the terrain texture is too even, the anatomy is too exact in places with five fingers and toes and teeth, the storm reads as a cotton wad, and the first step of the zoom is the largest and shows the most change. The build may fix any of these. Real atmosphere, cloud at many heights with shadows, and haze that thickens with distance are what sell scale from orbit.
- A viewer who is shown only the last frame at phone size must name a giant human body or skeleton at once. `kit/07-QUALITY-BAR.md` tests exactly that.
